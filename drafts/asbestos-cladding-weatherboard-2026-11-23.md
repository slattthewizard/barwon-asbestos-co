# Asbestos Cladding: Fibro Sheets, Fibro Weatherboards and the 10 Square Metre Problem

Walk down almost any older street in Geelong's northern suburbs and you'll see it: flat sheets with narrow strips covering the joins, or long planks that look like timber weatherboards from the street but aren't. Both can be asbestos cladding. If you own one of these houses, or you're about to buy or renovate one, the useful questions are how to tell, what ageing does to it, and what removal costs once you're past a patch repair.

## The two cladding styles to look for

**Flat sheets with cover strips.** This is the classic fibro look. Large flat panels are fixed to a frame, and the joins between them are covered with narrow battens or strips. Sheets are often painted, and the pattern of strips gives the wall a grid appearance.

**Fibro planks that imitate weatherboards.** These are long, narrow, overlapping boards with a shadow line along the bottom edge, made to look like timber weatherboard. People often assume they're timber until they knock on one. Timber sounds hollow and warm. Fibre cement tends to sound dull and hard, and it's much more brittle at the edges. That's a clue only, not a test. An asbestos weatherboard and a modern fibre-cement one can look almost the same.

Asbestos cement was commonly used in Australian housing from the mid-1940s until the late 1980s, and the total ban on all forms came into force on 31 December 2003. So a house built or extended before 1990 may have either style. If you're not sure what product you've got, our guide on [whether your fibro is asbestos, Hardiflex or Villaboard](/blog/is-my-fibro-asbestos-hardiflex-villaboard/) covers how to identify it. That article is the place for product names. This one is about the wall as a whole.

## Why Geelong has so much of it

Geelong's northern suburbs were built in bulk during the exact decades when asbestos cement was standard. The Housing Commission program in Norlane peaked from 1951 to 1957 and reached 2,464 houses by 1976, much of it for workers at Ford, International Harvester and a phosphate works. Corio gained five estates and 2,500 houses from the late 1950s over about twenty years. That timing is the local reason older cladding turns up so often here.

It doesn't tell you what's on your wall. Nobody has published how many of those houses contain asbestos, and extensions, re-clads and repairs mean any one house may have a mix. The only way to know is a lab test on a small sample.

## How asbestos cladding ages

Intact asbestos cement is a much lower risk than damaged material, because the fibres are bound into the cement. Ageing doesn't make a sound sheet suddenly dangerous, but it does change the odds of damage:

- Paint wears off, and the surface gets chalky or mossy.
- Edges chip and corners crumble, especially near the ground, where lawnmowers, whipper snippers and footballs do their work.
- Sheets crack from settling or from impact, and old fixing nails rust.
- Weather gets behind the cover strips and the lower edges, and the board softens or flakes.

Cracks and crumbling edges matter because broken material is how fibres get released. Our guide on [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) explains why a damaged or weathered sheet is treated more cautiously than a sound one. Never pressure-clean old cladding, and never scrub it dry with a wire brush. If you do see damage, keep kids and pets away from the spot and get the material tested.

## Why painting isn't a fix

It's tempting. A coat of paint makes the wall look sound and keeps weather out. It's also a trap, for three reasons.

First, preparation is the problem. To get paint to stick to old, chalky cladding you usually sand, scrape or wash it, and those are the very actions you shouldn't do to a suspect sheet.

Second, paint doesn't remove anything. It leaves the material exactly where it was, only now it's harder to identify, harder to inspect and harder to deal with later.

Third, it doesn't stop ageing. Edges and cracks still open up, and the paint film flakes off in time.

If the cladding is sound and you simply want to leave it alone, that can be a reasonable decision. It's still worth getting it tested so you know what you have, and then follow the cautions in our guide on [asbestos before renovating or demolishing in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-before-renovating-demolishing-victoria/). Painting is a different matter from preparing and sanding, and you should talk to a licensed removalist before anything abrasive happens.

## When re-cladding projects hit asbestos

This is where most cladding removal actually starts. Nobody wakes up wanting to remove the wall. It happens because of a renovation: an extension that needs a wall opened, new windows or doors, a re-clad to modernise the front, or insulation being added.

The moment a builder pulls a board off to see what's behind it, you've got a problem. If it's asbestos and it's broken, work should stop. Ideally you find out first. ## Why the area adds up so fast

Most homeowners underestimate how big a wall is.

In Victoria, work classed as "limited asbestos removal" (the kind that can be done without a licence) has two caps that both have to hold: no more than 10 square metres in total, and no more than one hour of removal work in a seven-day period. Those caps are workplace rules. They bind employers and self-employed people doing removal work. They don't bind a homeowner removing material at their own home, because the compliance code says a home is not regarded as a workplace, though WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the same code. They do become law if a tradesperson, tenant, rental property or paid helper is involved.

Even so, 10 square metres is not much cladding. Just one wall of an ordinary house can pass it, and a full set of external walls goes well beyond it. That's the real reason this work goes to a licensed removalist: it's simply a bigger job than a weekend. ## What cladding removal typically costs

Melbourne-metro benchmarks put general asbestos removal at $45 to $150 per square metre. There's no published Geelong-specific pricing, so use this as a starting point for a conversation, not a quote. Your price will depend on access, height, how much is damaged, whether it's single or double storey, and how far the removalist travels.

Every removal job also carries a minimum charge, typically $800 to $1,500, because containment, decontamination and disposal cost much the same however small the patch is. So if you're only removing a couple of damaged planks, you'll land near the minimum. Once you're stripping whole walls, the per-square-metre rate takes over.

Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST.

Testing is cheaper. A lab test typically costs $88 to $160 per sample, and most houses need three to eight samples because each different material is tested separately. That might mean the main wall sheets, the eaves and a laundry lining. A full house inspection with sampling generally runs $395 to $900, and standard turnaround is one to five business days. ## Paperwork and disposal

For non-friable removal totalling 10 square metres or less, the licensed removalist notifies WorkSafe at least 24 hours before starting. For larger jobs, which most cladding jobs are, it's at least five days. That's the straight answer to "why can't they start tomorrow."

Where non-friable material exceeds 10 square metres, a clearance certificate from an independent person is needed before the area is reoccupied. At your own home, the duty to obtain it sits with the licensed removalist you engaged, not with you. Disposal goes to a landfill authorised to receive asbestos, and the only one on EPA Victoria's register in the Barwon region is Drysdale Landfill. The removalist deals with disposal as part of the job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I tell if my weatherboards are fibro or timber?

You can't be certain by looking. Fibro planks are often harder, duller sounding and more brittle at the edges than timber, but modern fibre cement looks much the same as older asbestos cement. A lab test on a small sample is the only way to confirm what you have.

### Is it safe to paint over asbestos cladding?

Painting itself is not the main concern. The preparation is, because sanding, scraping or pressure-cleaning old cladding can release fibres. Paint also doesn't remove the material or stop it ageing. Get a sample tested first, and talk to a licensed removalist before doing any abrasive prep work.
