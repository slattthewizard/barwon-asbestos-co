# How Asbestos Testing Works in Victoria: Sampling, Labs and What the Report Says

The eaves lining usually comes down before anyone asks what it is made of. That is the ordinary sequence in a kitchen renovation on a 1970s brick veneer in Belmont, and it is the reason asbestos testing exists at all. A single sample analysed by a laboratory runs $88 to $160, while the minimum job charge a licensed removalist applies before anyone counts sheets sits at $800 to $1,500 on published Victorian rates.

Those two numbers are the whole argument for testing first. Here is how the process actually runs in Victoria, from the piece in the bag to the line on the report. Every figure below comes from published Victorian and national sources, and none of it is a quote from us.

## Why asbestos testing beats a confident guess

Asbestos cement was first manufactured in the 1920s and was standard in Australian residential building from the mid-1940s until the late 1980s. The total ban on manufacture, supply, use, import, transport and sale of all forms of asbestos only came into force on 31 December 2003. Anything fixed to a house inside that span might contain asbestos and might not.

Nothing on the surface of a sheet settles it. Fibro and modern fibre cement can sit side by side on the same wall after one renovation, weather the same way, and take paint the same way. Age narrows the odds, it does not answer the question.

The Victorian Government's position is that homes built in Australia until 1990 routinely used asbestos products such as cement sheeting and pipe insulation, and that asbestos is estimated to still be present in one in three homes. That is a national estimate. Nobody has published a figure for Geelong or the Barwon region, so treat any local percentage you see with suspicion.

The regional history explains the housing stock. The Housing Commission's Norlane program peaked between 1951 and 1957 and had delivered 2,464 houses by 1976, and Corio gained five estates of roughly 2,500 houses from the late 1950s onward. Ford, the Shell refinery from 1956 and Alcoa at Point Henry from 1963 were the employers who filled them. All of it went up while asbestos cement was the normal material, and none of it tells you about your particular sheet.

## How the sample is taken, and where it usually goes wrong

A sample is small. Someone cuts or breaks off a piece of the material, seals the cut edge, bags the piece, labels it and sends it to a lab. The laboratory work is the easy part of the day.

The risk sits in the thirty seconds before the bag closes. Dry cutting, sanding, drilling, grinding, water-blasting or snapping a sheet releases fibres, and that is usually the step people take before it occurs to them to ask the question. The material is left wet, disturbed as little as possible, and the sample comes from a spot where the damage is already done rather than a fresh cut across an intact sheet.

Each different material is its own sample. A fence, an eaves lining and a bathroom wall sheet are three samples, not one, because they were made at different times by different manufacturers and one result says nothing about the others. Most houses need three to eight samples, and larger properties run to eight or twelve.

Take the Belmont kitchen from the top of the page. The eaves lining, the sheet behind the old cupboards and the lining in the laundry are three separate submissions. At $88 to $160 per sample that is a test bill in the low hundreds. Labs price this two ways: some charge a flat rate with no volume break, others drop the rate from the second sample onward. Ask which model applies before you send five pieces.

Whether you take the sample or somebody takes it for you, the honest position from Asbestos Victoria is that removal work is best performed by licensed asbestos removalists who are trained to do it safely. Sampling is not removal, but the same caution applies to the material.

## What an approved lab does with your asbestos sample

Victoria's OHS Regulations are narrow on this point. Only an approved asbestos laboratory can analyse samples to identify asbestos, and approved means approved by NATA to identify asbestos in samples or to perform fibre counting, or operating under a scheme determined by WorkSafe. Neither a builder's opinion nor a confident neighbour counts.

At the lab the sample is examined under a microscope, and the fibres are identified by how they behave optically rather than by how the sheet looked on the wall. The result is a written report, and endorsed reports carry the NATA insignia. You can check whether a laboratory holds that accreditation at nata.com.au before you post anything to it.

Standard turnaround published by Victorian and national labs runs 1 to 5 business days. If you are holding up a trade, faster options exist: labs publish 24 to 48 hour results for a surcharge of roughly 25% to 50% on their normal rate. That is a lab surcharge, not a promise anybody can make on their behalf, and weekend cut-offs are common.

One practical note for the Bellarine and the Surf Coast. Posting a sample to a mail-in lab adds transit days that nobody counts at the start, so if a demolition date or a settlement is driving the timeline, say so up front and choose the service accordingly.

## What the asbestos testing report says, and what it does not

A report is a narrow document, and that is its strength. It records the sample reference, a description of the material as received, and whether asbestos was detected in that sample. Where asbestos is present, it names the type, either by the common colour names or by the mineral names you will see written as chrysotile, amosite and crocidolite.

What it does not do is clear your house. The result applies to the piece submitted and nothing else. A negative on the eaves says nothing about the wall sheet two metres away, which is exactly why the three to eight sample range exists.

It also does not tell you what to do next. A report is not a removal plan, not a price, and not a clearance certificate. Those are separate things with separate rules, and the clearance certificate in particular has a legal definition in Victoria that catches people out.

Vinyl floor tiles in an Ocean Grove holiday house are a good example of the report's limits. The tiles come up under old carpet, they are a suspicious vintage, and one sample at $88 to $160 answers whether asbestos is present in the tile submitted. The backing and the adhesive under it are a separate question. Whether the material counts as friable is a judgement about its condition that a licensed removalist makes on site, and neither of those is settled by the line on the report. Ask for the tile and the bedding material to be treated as separate samples if the floor is coming up.

## What a positive result changes on site

This is where most asbestos content goes wrong, so here it is stated properly. The 10 square metre cap and the one hour in any seven days cap come from the OHS Regulations, and both must hold for non-friable material to be removed without a licence. The hour is cumulative across jobs, not per job, and friable material is never covered by that exemption at any quantity.

Those regulations bind employers and self-employed persons at workplaces. WorkSafe's compliance code is explicit that where someone removes asbestos in domestic premises that are their own home, the OHS Regulations do not apply, because the home is not regarded as a workplace. WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the same code anyway.

The moment a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property, a business premises or a paid helper enters the picture, those caps are law rather than guidance. Our guide on [removing asbestos yourself in Victoria](/blog/can-i-remove-asbestos-myself-victoria/) works through that distinction, and [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) explains why the friable answer changes everything.

A Super Six garage roof in Corio that has started to crack shows what the arithmetic does. Test a sheet or two first at $88 to $160 each. If it comes back positive, the roof is far past 10 square metres and far past an hour of work, which is the honest reason that job goes to a licensed removalist rather than a weekend. Class B licence holders cover non-friable material, Class A is required for friable material, and the WorkSafe Victoria licence number appears on the quote either way.

One piece of good news for homeowners. Above 10 square metres a clearance certificate is required before the area is re-occupied, and where a homeowner has engaged the licence holder directly at domestic premises, the duty to obtain that certificate sits on the licence holder, not on you. It has to come from an independent person who is not the removalist and has no financial interest in the job, which is why it shows as a separate line. [Clearance certificates and air monitoring](/blog/asbestos-clearance-certificate-air-monitoring/) covers when each one applies.

Disposal is the other thing a positive result sets in motion. Exactly one facility in the Barwon region is on EPA Victoria's register of landfills licensed to accept asbestos waste, Drysdale Landfill, on (03) 5251 2935, and it takes small domestic quantities only up to a 6x4 trailer. Every Surf Coast Shire transfer station refuses asbestos outright, so a Torquay or Anglesea load has one legal destination and it is on the Bellarine. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does asbestos testing cost in Victoria?

Published rates run $88 to $160 per sample, and most houses need three to eight samples because every different material has to be tested separately. An on-site inspection with sampling is a Melbourne-metro benchmark of $395 to $900. There is no published Geelong rate for either, so ask what the figure covers and whether it includes GST.

### How long do asbestos test results take?

Victorian and national laboratories publish a standard turnaround of 1 to 5 business days from when the sample reaches them. A 24 to 48 hour result is usually available for a surcharge of about 25% to 50%. Weekend cut-offs are common, so a Friday sample often means a Monday start, and posting time sits on top of all of that.

### Can I take the asbestos sample myself?

There is no law stopping a homeowner from taking a sample at their own home, and WorkSafe recommends following the same compliance code that binds workplaces. The risk is in the disturbance rather than the piece itself, so nothing gets cut dry, sanded, drilled or snapped. If the material is already damaged, crumbling or in poor condition, leave it and ask a licensed removalist to look at it first.

### Does one negative result mean the house is clear?

No. A result applies only to the sample submitted, which is why a fence, an eaves lining and a bathroom sheet count as three tests rather than one. Materials from different eras sit side by side in most renovated houses. If you are planning to disturb several areas, sample each of them.
