## 0. Non-negotiable wording rules

Every copywriter reads this section first. These rules bind every page and every article.

- The site holds **NO licence and claims none**. Removal is always attributed: "carried out by
  licensed asbestos removalists we work with — their WorkSafe Victoria licence number appears on
  every quote."
- Never state or imply the business is a licensed removalist, licensed asbestos assessor, or
  NATA-accredited. Never invent a licence number, ABN, staff, trucks, case studies or
  testimonials.
- **VICTORIA-SPECIFIC:** never say we "arrange", "organise", "manage", "co-ordinate" or "take
  care of" the removal. The site is advertising that puts homeowners in touch with licensed
  removalists; the homeowner contracts directly with the removalist. Safe phrasing: "get a quote
  from a licensed removalist", "we'll put you in touch with". This exists because Victoria's
  Domestic Building Contracts Act s 5(1)(ga) makes "managing or arranging" domestic building work
  itself domestic building work.
- Never offer the replacement work that follows removal (re-cladding, re-roofing, a new fence,
  repairs). That IS domestic building work. Replacement is always something the homeowner
  arranges separately with a registered builder.

### 0.1 The primary source behind the Victorian rule

Verified verbatim from the authorised text of the Act (version 094, in force 1 July 2026,
Chief Parliamentary Counsel), **Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995 (Vic) s 5(1)**:

> (d) the demolition or removal of a home;
>
> (f) any site work (including work required to gain access, or to remove impediments to access,
> to a site) related to work referred to in paragraphs (a) to (e);
>
> (g) the preparation of plans or specifications for the carrying out of work referred to in
> paragraphs (a) to (f);
>
> **(ga) the managing or arranging of the carrying out of work referred to in paragraphs (a) to (g);**

The Act's own marginal note records: `S. 5(1)(ga) inserted by No. 4/2024 s. 4.` That amendment
commenced 28 February 2024.

Section 6 lists the work the Act does *not* apply to — farm buildings, buildings used only for
business purposes, buildings to accommodate animals, architect/registered-designer design work,
foundations data, and transporting a building between sites. **There is no asbestos exemption in
s 6.** Do not tell anyone there is one.

- Act landing page (version history): https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/domestic-building-contracts-act-1995
- Authorised PDF used for the quotes above: https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/95-91aa094-authorised.pdf

## 1. The Victorian legal thresholds

Victoria is **not** a model-WHS state. It runs its own Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004
and Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017. Terms that are correct in NSW or Queensland
are wrong here — most importantly, Victoria has **no "licensed asbestos assessor"** licence
class. Never use that phrase on this site. Victoria's term is "independent person".

Unless noted, every quote below is verbatim from **WorkSafe Victoria, Compliance code: Removing
asbestos in workplaces, Edition 2, December 2019**, which carries the regulation references
inline. Landing page: https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/resources/compliance-code-removing-asbestos-workplaces

### 1.1 The unlicensed limit — area cap AND time cap

This is the single most important legal fact on the site, and the one people get wrong by copying
NSW content. **Victoria has a time element that NSW does not.**

Compliance code paragraph 35 and Table 1, citing **OHS Regulations r250**:

> An asbestos removal licence is not required where non-friable ACM or ACD is permitted to be
> removed as limited asbestos removal work

> Limited asbestos removal work includes:
> - the removal of non-friable ACM if —
>   - **the area of non-friable ACM to be removed does not exceed 10 square metres in total, and**
>   - **the employer or self-employed person does not perform more than one hour of asbestos
>     removal work in total during a seven day period**, or
> - the removal of ACD if —
>   - the asbestos removal work does not exceed 10 minutes in total, and
>   - the employer or self-employed person does not perform more than one hour of asbestos removal
>     work in total during a seven day period, or
> - the removal of ACD if an independent person has determined that airborne asbestos fibre levels
>   are likely to be less than one half of the asbestos exposure standard (0.05 f/ml)

Source (same wording, plain page): https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/builders-and-trades/asbestos-removalists/unlicensed-asbestos-removal

> "the area of asbestos-containing material to be removed does not exceed 10 square metres in
> total, and the total time asbestos removal work is performed in any period of seven days does
> not exceed one hour"

**Two caps, both of which must hold.** Both caps are per seven-day period, and the hour is
cumulative across jobs, not per job. Friable material is never covered by this exemption.

**The nuance that must be stated accurately when writing about DIY.** The OHS Regulations bind
employers and self-employed persons at workplaces. Compliance code paragraph 34:

> Where asbestos removal is being performed by a person in domestic premises which is their own
> home, the OHS Regulations do not apply because the home is not regarded as a workplace. It is
> recommended that this Code be used to minimise the risks associated with asbestos removal work
> in such instances.

So the site must **not** say "it is illegal for a homeowner to remove more than 10 square metres
at their own home". What it may say, and what is accurate: the 10 m² / one-hour figure is the
limit the law sets for anyone doing this as work; WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the same
code at their own home; and the moment a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property, a business
premises or a paid helper is involved, the caps are law. Asbestos Victoria's own homeowner page
puts it this way:

> "Asbestos removal work is best performed by licensed asbestos removalists, who are
> appropriately trained to perform the removal work safely."

https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/in-the-home/find-manage-remove-dispose/homeowner-removal

A full fence line, a whole garage or a house roof is far past 10 m² and far past one hour. That
is the honest reason those jobs go to a licensed removalist, and it is the reason to give.

### 1.2 Class A versus Class B

Verbatim from https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/builders-and-trades/asbestos-removalists

> "Class A licence-holders are permitted to perform Class B and Class A asbestos removal work,
> which means asbestos removal work (other than limited asbestos removal work) involving the
> removal of: 1. friable asbestos, or 2. asbestos-contaminated dust (other than
> asbestos-contaminated dust associated with or derived from the removal of non-friable asbestos)."

> "Class B licence holders are only permitted to perform Class B asbestos removal work, which
> means asbestos removal work (other than limited asbestos removal work) involving the removal of:
> 1. non-friable asbestos, or 2. asbestos-contaminated dust associated with or derived from the
> removal of non-friable asbestos."

In plain terms for copy: **Class A covers friable asbestos (and everything a Class B can do).
Class B covers non-friable asbestos only.** Almost all residential work in Geelong — fibro
cladding, fences, Super Six roofing, eaves, shed and garage sheeting — is non-friable and is
Class B work. Friable material (pipe lagging, some old insulation, fire-damaged or crumbling
sheeting, asbestos-contaminated dust) is Class A and **always** requires a licensed removalist,
with no exemption at any quantity.

Licence application fees, for reference only — do not publish these as consumer prices:
Class A $1,307.30 and Class B $1,113.90.
https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/asbestos-removal-licences

### 1.3 Notifying WorkSafe before the job

Compliance code paragraphs 276, 278 and 279, citing **OHS Regulations r298**:

> An asbestos removal licence holder must notify WorkSafe before commencing asbestos removal work.

> For asbestos removal work involving a total area of 10 square metres or less of non-friable ACM
> that is present in a building, structure, ship or plant, the asbestos removal licence holder
> must notify WorkSafe **at least 24 hours** before the proposed work commences.

> For any other asbestos removal work, the asbestos removal licence holder must notify WorkSafe
> **at least five days** before the proposed work commences.

This is useful, publishable context for "why can't they start tomorrow" content. The duty sits on
the licence holder, never on us and never on the homeowner.

### 1.4 Who may do air monitoring and issue clearance certificates

**Victoria uses "independent person", not "licensed asbestos assessor".** Compliance code
paragraph 471, citing **OHS Regulations r207(1)**:

> For the purpose of 'Part 4.4 — Asbestos' of the OHS Regulations, an independent person is a
> person who, in relation to the carrying out of a relevant function:
> - is independent from the following:
>   - the employer or self-employed person performing the asbestos removal work
>   - the asbestos removal licence holder performing asbestos removal work, and
>   - the person who commissioned the asbestos removal work
> - does not have a conflict of interest in carrying out the relevant function, and
> - has the requisite knowledge, skills and experience to carry out the relevant function.

Paragraph 472 (**r207(2)**) defines the three relevant functions: determining airborne asbestos
fibre levels, the visual inspection of an area for visible asbestos residue, and the giving of a
clearance certificate. Paragraph 474 adds: "An independent person cannot be a director, employee
or person with a pecuniary interest."

**When a clearance certificate is required.** Paragraphs 463 and 464:

> The person who commissioned Class A or Class B asbestos removal work must obtain a clearance
> certificate from an independent person on completion of the asbestos removal work and before the
> area where the asbestos removal work was being performed is re-occupied. **OHS Regulations r297(1)**

> A clearance certificate is not required if the asbestos removal work consisted only of the
> removal of non-friable asbestos-containing material **not exceeding 10 square metres in total**.
> **OHS Regulations r297(3)**

**Who must obtain it for a homeowner.** Paragraph 466, and the same rule restated on WorkSafe's
own page at https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/resources/general-requirements-all-asbestos-removal-work

> The person who commissioned Class A or Class B asbestos removal work (not the asbestos removal
> licence holder) must obtain a clearance certificate. The exception to this is when removal is
> conducted at domestic premises used solely for domestic purposes (and the person who commissioned
> the removal is the owner of the premises). In this case, the asbestos removal licence holder must
> obtain the clearance certificate.

> "Where the removal is being performed at a domestic premises and the asbestos removal licence
> holder has been engaged by the homeowner, the duty to obtain a clearance certificate is placed on
> the licence holder."

That is a genuinely good, publishable fact: **for a Geelong homeowner, the removalist carries the
clearance duty, not you.**

**Air monitoring.** Paragraph 454:

> The OHS Regulations do not require paraoccupational air monitoring for Class B asbestos removal
> work.

Paragraph 459 (**r294(2)**) requires it on completion of Class A work where it was required, "to
verify that the airborne asbestos fibre level is less than 0.01 f/ml". Paragraph 462: where levels
are equal to or above 0.01 f/ml "a clearance certificate cannot be issued and further
decontamination ... needs to take place".

So: **air monitoring is a Class A (friable) requirement, not a routine part of a residential fence
or roof job.** Never imply every job gets air monitoring.

**Who analyses the samples.** Paragraph 446, citing **OHS Regulations r213(1)** and **r5**:

> Only an approved asbestos laboratory can analyse samples to identify asbestos. An approved
> asbestos laboratory means a laboratory approved by NATA to perform asbestos fibre counting or to
> identify asbestos in samples, or a scheme determined by WorkSafe.

Paragraph 448: "The NATA website (nata.com.au) can be used to confirm if a laboratory is approved
to perform asbestos fibre counting." Endorsed reports carry the NATA insignia. This is the correct,
defensible way to describe our testing service: **the sample is analysed by a NATA-accredited
laboratory.** It is never a claim that we are accredited.

### 1.5 Disposal: where it may go, and how it must be wrapped, labelled and carried

**Classification.** https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/asbestos-waste

> "Asbestos waste is pre-classified as reportable priority waste under Schedule 5 of the
> Environment Protection Regulations 2021."

**Where it may go.** https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/manage-asbestos-waste and
https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/dispose-asbestos-waste

> "You must make sure that asbestos waste is taken to a landfill authorised to receive it."

> "Not all landfills are authorised to receive asbestos waste."

> "We have a register of landfills in Victoria that can legally accept asbestos."

> "Contact the landfill before you transport and deliver your household asbestos. This is because:
> landfills authorised to receive asbestos can still choose not to accept it; you may have to book
> a time ahead so that staff are ready to store your asbestos waste safely."

> "Domestic asbestos landfills will only accept a small amount of household asbestos waste."

The register is an on-page table headed "Landfills licensed for asbestos waste", stamped **updated
22 June 2026**, with 26 entries statewide. See section 3 for what that means for the Barwon region.

**Packaging and labelling.** https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/in-the-home/find-manage-remove-dispose/homeowner-removal/packaging-asbestos

- Polythene sheeting "approximately 200 μm (0.2 mm) thick", sealed with adhesive tape.
- Friable material and dust go in "double polythene bags approximately 200 μm (0.2 mm) thick".
- Bagged dust: "loaded weight should not exceed 30 kg" and "Bags should only be filled to be half
  full".
- Every package carries the asbestos warning mark, approximately "75 mm X 90 mm", reading:
  **"CAUTION ASBESTOS DO NOT OPEN OR DAMAGE BAG DO NOT INHALE DUST"**.
- For asbestos cement sheeting in a vehicle: "place two layers of polythene sheeting ... in the
  cargo-carrying compartment", with packages "small enough to be handled easily".
- "All containers must display the asbestos warning mark before transport."

**Transport permissions.** EPA requires a permission to transport reportable priority waste for
industrial/commercial asbestos loads (permission types A10a for high-risk transport and A10b
otherwise), with movements recorded through EPA's Waste Tracker. A householder taking a small
domestic load to an authorised landfill is a different case and does not need a transport
permission — but the packaging, labelling and "call ahead" rules above still apply.

**One superseded-source trap.** EPA publication **IWRG611.2, "Asbestos transport and disposal",
dated 26 June 2017** (https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/iwrg6112-asbestos-transport-and-disposal) is
still online and still turns up first in search. It predates the Environment Protection Act 2017
regime that commenced 1 July 2021 and it uses the old vocabulary: "prescribed industrial waste
(PIW)" and "Category C waste". Its packaging advice is consistent with the current guidance, but
**never quote its waste-classification wording on the site.** The current term is *reportable
priority waste*.

### How to read this section

- Every figure is in **AUD** and carries its source URL and a VERIFIED or ESTIMATE tag.
- **VERIFIED** = published by a named Australian supplier on their own rate card, by an Australian
  council or government body, or by a reputable Australian cost aggregator (labelled as such).
- **ESTIMATE** = a band derived here from two or more verified rows. Every ESTIMATE row says how it
  was derived. Nothing else in this file is derived.

- The **Region** column matters. There is **no published Geelong or Barwon pricing anywhere** — see

  regional work separately. Frame Victorian figures as Melbourne-metro benchmarks, never as "Geelong
  prices".

## 4. Local grounding facts

Only facts with a source. Everything here was fetched and read.

### 4.1 When asbestos cement was used, and when it was banned

From Asbestos Victoria, https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/about-asbestos/what-is-asbestos

> "asbestos cement materials were first manufactured in the 1920's. These were commonly used in the
> manufacture of residential and commercial building materials from the mid-1940's until the late
> 1980's"

> "Australia banned the use and import of blue asbestos, brown asbestos and asbestos-containing
> products in the mid-1980s. The manufacture and import of white asbestos products was banned in
> December 2003."

> "From 31 December 2003, the total ban on manufacture, supply, use, reuse, import, transport,
> storage and sale of all forms of asbestos came into force."

> "Asbestos mining ended in Australia by 1983"

**The prevalence figure the site may use**, from the Premier of Victoria's media release of
21 November 2023, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/fight-protect-workers-asbestos-never-ends

> "Homes built in Australian until 1990 routinely used asbestos products such as cement sheeting
> and thermal insulation for pipes."

> "Asbestos is estimated to still be present in one in three homes."

**That is a national figure.** There is no verified Geelong or Barwon prevalence figure — see 5.9.
Never write "one in three Geelong homes".

**The practical rule for copy**, and it is defensible from the above: a house built or extended
before 1990 may contain asbestos, and the mid-1940s to late-1980s window is the high-risk period.
Never give a single "if your house is older than X" year as though it were a legal test.

### 4.2 Why the Geelong region is asbestos-heavy

The region's suburbs were built by industry, in exactly the decades asbestos cement was standard.

**Ford, Norlane.** From the Victorian Heritage Database entry for the Ford Motor Company Complex,
Norlane, https://vhd.heritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/places/12548

> "The Ford Motor Company Complex in Norlane, a suburb of Geelong ... The buildings were constructed
> in stages between 1925 and 1942."

> "A 40 hectare site was purchased from the Geelong Harbour Trust in March 1925 and the two
> buildings were completed in 1926."

> "From 1934 up to 1942, both buildings were extended eastwards in the same configuration. Ford
> developed military vehicles at Geelong for the Defence Department from 1936, and commenced
> large-scale production of military vehicles and armaments in 1939. By the company's fiftieth
> anniversary in 1953, Ford was Geelong's biggest employer with over 5000 workers"

> "In 1959, assembly operations were moved to a major new plant at Campbellfield, north of
> Melbourne, and the Australian headquarters was shifted there in 1961."

**Norlane's Housing Commission estate.** From Victorian Places (Monash University and the University
of Queensland), https://www.victorianplaces.com.au/norlane

> "the Housing Commission began its house-building program in Norlane"

> "The peak building period was 1951-57."

> "Over 1200 houses were prefabricated units imported from Holland and France."

> "By 1976 when its program was completed there were 2464 Commission houses."

> "Much of the housing was for employees at Ford, International Harvester and a phosphate works."

**Corio's estates.** From Victorian Places, https://www.victorianplaces.com.au/corio

> "During the late 1950s the Housing Commission built the first of its Corio estates. Within about
> 20 years the Commission built five estates comprising 2500 houses at Corio."

> "The largest development was the Shell Oil Refinery (1956)"

Corio's census population: 95 in 1911, 857 in 1947, 15,342 in 2001. The growth is entirely
post-war.

**Alcoa Point Henry.** From the ABC's fiftieth-anniversary timeline,
https://www.abc.net.au/news/2014-12-18/timeline:-50-years-of-operation-at-alcoa/5977286

> "The ground was broken at Point Henry for Alcoa's smelter and semi-fabricating plant" (1961)

> "Alcoa opened its smelter at Point Henry, east of Geelong" (1963); "The first ingot was poured in
> April 1963."

The rolling mill opened in 1965. The smelter closed in August 2014 and the rolling mill in December
2014.

### 4.3 The joined-up fact, and how to say it

Put the two verified timelines side by side and the point makes itself, with no invention required:

- Asbestos cement was standard in Australian residential building from the **mid-1940s to the late
  1980s** (4.1).
- Geelong's northern suburbs were built in bulk inside that window: the Norlane Commission program
  peaked **1951-57** and ran to **2,464 houses by 1976**; Corio gained **five estates and 2,500
  houses** from the late 1950s over about twenty years; Ford, International Harvester, the Shell
  refinery (1956) and Alcoa Point Henry (1963) were the employers that filled them.

That is the whole local angle, and every number in it is sourced. **Never extend it into a claim
about how many of those houses actually contain asbestos** — nobody has published that figure.
