# Asbestos Register and Management Plan: Does a Victorian Home Need One?

If you've been reading about asbestos for a while, you'll have come across two documents that sound official: an asbestos register and an asbestos management plan. Real estate agents mention them, tradies ask about them, and some websites make it sound like every house in Victoria must have one.

That isn't how it works. Both documents come out of the workplace safety rules, and a private home isn't a workplace. That said, keeping your own record of what you've found and tested at your place is a smart habit, and it'll save you, your tradies and a future buyer a lot of guessing. Here's the plain version.

## What an asbestos register is

An asbestos register is a written record of where asbestos or suspected asbestos is located in a building, what it is, what condition it's in, and what's been done about it. Think of it as a map plus a list: this sheet of eaves lining at the back, tested and confirmed, in fair condition. That wall in the laundry, not tested, assumed to contain asbestos until proven otherwise.

A register is a living document. When you test something new, remove something, or find something you hadn't spotted, the record changes. A register that was accurate five years ago can be wrong today, which is the main reason people keep them up to date.

The point of a register is simple: anybody who's about to work on the building should be able to find out what's in it before they put a drill through it.

## What an asbestos management plan is

An asbestos management plan builds on the register. Where the register says what's there and where, the plan says what's going to be done about it. It covers things like how the material will be kept in good condition, who needs to be told, how work near it will be controlled, and what happens if it's damaged.

The register is the inventory, and the plan is the approach. Both are part of the workplace rules.

## They're workplace duties

This is the part that causes confusion. Registers and management plans are duties under Victoria's occupational health and safety framework, which is about workplaces. Victoria has its own Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 and Regulations 2017, and it doesn't follow the model laws used in some other states. A lot of articles written for New South Wales or Queensland use terms that don't apply here.

WorkSafe's own compliance code says that where a person is working with asbestos at their own home, the OHS Regulations don't apply, because the home isn't regarded as a workplace. WorkSafe recommends using the code anyway to reduce the risks. So a private home that's lived in by its owner doesn't legally need a register or a management plan.

That's the legal answer. It isn't a reason to ignore the idea.

## Why you should keep your own record anyway

You don't need a formal document, but a simple folder is worth the effort. Here's what goes in it.

- **Test reports.** Every lab result from a NATA-accredited laboratory, with the date and the sample location. If the report says no asbestos detected, keep that too. It stops someone retesting the same fence in five years.
- **A rough map or list of where material is.** A hand-drawn floor plan or a phone note works: "eaves, all sides, tested positive", "back shed roof, not tested", "bathroom wall sheet, tested, no asbestos".
- **Photos.** Date-stamped photos of the material before anything is done to it, and after. Good for your records and for any quote.
- **Work history.** If a removalist has been through, keep the quote, the invoice, and any paperwork they give you. If anything was left in place, write down where.
- **Notes on condition.** A cracked sheet today might be a crumbling sheet in three years. A dated note makes the change obvious.

Who benefits? Pretty much everyone. A tradie quoting for electrical or plumbing work wants to know what's in the walls before they start. A buyer's inspector will ask. ## Where the workplace rules might still apply

Plenty of properties aren't a simple owner-occupied home. If any of these describe your situation, the workplace rules may apply, and the safest move is to check with WorkSafe Victoria before you assume otherwise.

**A home-based business.** If you run a business from your house, such as a studio, a workshop or a clinic, part of the property may be a workplace. Employees, contractors or customers coming to the premises can change the picture.

**A rental property.** Once a tenant, a landlord or a paid tradie is involved, you're no longer looking at an owner working at their own home. Our guide to [asbestos in a rental property](/blog/asbestos-rental-property-victoria/) goes through what that means for both sides.

**A farm shed used for work.** A shed that's used for the business of the farm, with employees or contractors using it, may be a workplace. A shed that just holds the family's camping gear is a different matter, and the line between them isn't always obvious.

**A strata common area.** The shared parts of a building, like stairwells, roofs, car parks, laundries and fences, aren't one owner's own home. If you're on an owners corporation committee, don't assume the home rule covers you. Ask WorkSafe Victoria and your strata manager.

There's no point quoting regulations at any of these, because the answer depends on who's using the premises and how, and a general article can't settle that. A short call to WorkSafe Victoria is the right way to find out.

## What to do with the record once you have it

Hand it to anyone who's about to work on the building, before they start. Give a copy to a removalist when you ask for a quote. If you sell, put it in the sale pack. If you rent the place out, give a copy to the tenant and the agent.

When things change, update it. If you remove the old fence, write it down. If you find another material, test it and add the result. And if you're unsure about a material, treat it as suspect until you know.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does my house need an asbestos register in Victoria?

Not legally, if it's your own home and a private residence. Registers and management plans are workplace duties under Victoria's occupational health and safety framework, and WorkSafe's compliance code says a home isn't regarded as a workplace. Keeping your own record is still sensible, and it helps tradies, buyers and tenants.

### What's the difference between an asbestos register and a management plan?

A register records where asbestos or suspected asbestos is, what it is and what condition it's in. A management plan covers what's going to be done about it. The register is the inventory and the plan is the approach.

### What should I put in my own asbestos records at home?

Lab test reports, a simple list or sketch of where materials are, dated photos, quotes and invoices from any removal work, and notes on the condition of anything left in place. Update it whenever something changes.

### When might the workplace rules apply to a property that looks like a home?

Home-based businesses, rental properties, farm sheds used for work and strata common areas can all raise the question. Check with WorkSafe Victoria rather than assuming, because the answer depends on how the premises are used and who's there.
