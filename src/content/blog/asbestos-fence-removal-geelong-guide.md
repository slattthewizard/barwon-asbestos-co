## How to tell whether a fibro fence is asbestos

Age is the first filter. Asbestos cement was used in Australian residential building from the mid 1940s until the late 1980s, and the total ban on all forms of asbestos came into force on 31 December 2003. A fence built inside that window is worth treating as asbestos until a laboratory says otherwise. A fence built well after it almost certainly is not, because the modern fibre cement sold since the ban looks similar and contains no asbestos.

That similarity is the whole problem. You cannot settle this by eye, and neither can a tradesperson, no matter how confident they sound. Flat grey sheeting between timber or concrete posts is the classic fibro fence, but plenty of harmless sheeting looks the same from three metres away. Only analysis of a physical sample resolves it.

## What testing costs, and how many samples you actually need

Published Victorian rates run $88 to $160 per sample. Two pricing models exist and they do not blend: some labs charge one flat fee per sample no matter how many you send, and others discount from the second sample onward. If you have several materials to check, ask which model the lab runs.

Most houses need three to eight samples, because every different material has to be tested separately. A fence, an eaves lining and a bathroom wall sheet are three samples, not one. Larger properties run to eight or twelve. If you would rather have someone walk the property and sample it, published Victorian rates for a house inspection run $395 to $900.

Standard laboratory turnaround runs one to five business days, and a 24 to 48 hour result usually carries a surcharge of roughly 25% to 50%. ## Can you pull the fence down yourself?

This is where most asbestos content gets Victoria wrong, usually by copying New South Wales material. Victoria runs its own Occupational Health and Safety Regulations, and they carry a time cap that other states do not have.

Under regulation 250, non friable asbestos may be removed without a licence only where the area to be removed does not exceed 10 square metres in total, and the employer or self employed person does not perform more than one hour of asbestos removal work in total during a seven day period. Both caps have to hold, and the hour is cumulative across jobs rather than per job. The plain language version is on [Asbestos Victoria's unlicensed removal page](https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/builders-and-trades/asbestos-removalists/unlicensed-asbestos-removal).

Where that rule applies is the part people miss. Those regulations bind employers and self employed persons at workplaces. WorkSafe's compliance code says at paragraph 34 that where removal is performed by a person in domestic premises which is their own home, the OHS Regulations do not apply, because the home is not regarded as a workplace, and it recommends the code be used anyway.

So it is not accurate to say it is illegal for a homeowner to remove more than 10 square metres at their own home. What is accurate: that is the limit the law sets for anyone doing it as work, WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the same code, and the caps become law the moment a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property, a business premises or a paid helper is involved.

For a fence, the arithmetic tends to answer the question anyway. A full boundary fence line is far past 10 square metres and far past an hour of work, so it sits outside the unlicensed exemption for anyone doing it as work.

Friable material is never covered at any quantity and always needs a Class A removalist. Class A covers friable asbestos and everything Class B can do; Class B covers non friable only. Intact fibro fencing is non friable Class B work, but sheeting that has gone crumbly or been through a fire is a different category. ## What asbestos fence removal involves on the day

A licensed removalist has to notify WorkSafe before starting. For work involving a total area of 10 square metres or less of non friable material, the notice period is at least 24 hours. For anything larger, it is at least five days. That duty sits on the licence holder, never on you, and it is the honest reason nobody can start tomorrow morning.

Sheets come off whole wherever possible, because breaking them is what releases fibres. They are wrapped in polythene approximately 200 microns thick and sealed with tape, and every package carries the asbestos warning mark, roughly 75mm by 90mm, reading CAUTION ASBESTOS DO NOT OPEN OR DAMAGE BAG DO NOT INHALE DUST. That labelling is what makes the load legal to move and what the landfill expects to see when it arrives.

Above 10 square metres a clearance certificate is required, and it has to come from an independent person. That is Victoria's term, not the New South Wales phrase "licensed asbestos assessor", which does not exist as a licence class here. The independent person cannot be a director or employee of the removalist and cannot hold a pecuniary interest in the job, which is why clearance is a separate line on a quote. Published Victorian rates for a visual clearance certificate on a typical residential job run $165 to $250.

Take a 1960s fibro fence line along a back boundary in Norlane. It is well past both caps, so it is a licensed job: the published whole fence band applies and a clearance certificate is a separate line.

The posts and the new fence are a separate matter again. Replacing a fence is domestic building work, so you take that up directly with a registered builder or fencing contractor. We do not price it.

## Where the sheets are allowed to go

This part is genuinely local, and it catches people out. Asbestos waste is pre classified as reportable priority waste in Victoria, and it may only go to a landfill authorised to receive it. EPA Victoria keeps the statutory register of those sites, and you can check it yourself on the [EPA's asbestos disposal page](https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/dispose-asbestos-waste).

Exactly one facility in the entire Barwon region is on that register: Drysdale Landfill, run by the City of Greater Geelong, on (03) 5251 2935. It takes small domestic quantities only, to a maximum of a six by four trailer. Fees apply, though the amount is not published anywhere we could verify, so ring and ask. EPA's guidance is to contact the landfill before you transport and deliver, because an authorised landfill can still decline a load and you may need to book a time.

Every Surf Coast Shire transfer station refuses asbestos outright, which covers Anglesea, Winchelsea, Torquay and Lorne. Colac Otway Shire publishes no asbestos disposal option at all, and no commercial receiver serves the Geelong area. So a homeowner in Torquay with two wrapped sheets in the trailer has one legal destination in the region, and it is on the Bellarine. Outside the region, Ballarat Regional Landfill takes asbestos by appointment only, Thursdays between 10am and 12pm, on 03 5342 8540.

For context, other Victorian councils, not Geelong, publish asbestos gate fees between $100 and $376 per tonne. That is nearly a fourfold spread across three shires, which is why a single Victorian disposal figure would mislead.
