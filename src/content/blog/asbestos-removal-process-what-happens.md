Most people have never had asbestos removed, so the process is a bit of a mystery. Who turns up, what do they do, and why can't it happen this week? Here's the whole sequence for a typical non-friable fibro job in Victoria, in the order it happens. Some steps are done by you, most by the licensed removalist, and a few by third parties.

## Step 1: Testing

Nothing starts until you know the material is asbestos. You can't tell by looking. A sample goes to a NATA-accredited laboratory, which analyses it. Each different material needs its own sample, so most houses need three to eight. Standard turnaround is about one to five business days, and a 24 to 48 hour result costs a 25% to 50% surcharge. A lab test is typically $88 to $160 per sample in Melbourne-metro pricing. Our guide to [how asbestos testing works in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-testing-how-it-works-victoria/) goes through the sampling and the lab report, and our [Geelong testing page](/asbestos-testing-geelong/) covers booking one. If you'd like help, call (03) 5550 4817, or [book a test](/#contact).

## Step 2: Getting quotes

If a sample comes back positive, ask licensed removalists for written quotes. Each should show the WorkSafe Victoria licence number and class, the material and area, the disposal route, whether a clearance certificate is included and who obtains it, and the minimum job charge. Melbourne-metro benchmarks put the minimum at $800 to $1,500. Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST. You contract directly with the removalist you choose. We'll put you in touch with licensed removalists we work with: [get a quote from a licensed removalist](/#contact).

## Step 3: WorkSafe notification

Before work starts, the licence holder must notify WorkSafe Victoria. For 10 m2 or less of non-friable material, the notice must be given at least 24 hours before the work begins. For any other removal work, it's at least five days. This is the real reason nobody can start tomorrow on a bigger job: the law builds a waiting period in. The duty belongs to the removalist, not to you.

## How long does asbestos removal take?

Honestly, there's no published figure for the duration of a typical job, and it depends on the scope: how much material, how it's fixed, how easy it is to reach, and whether it's wall sheeting, a roof or a shed. The only hard numbers are the notification lead times above. After that, ask each removalist for a time estimate in writing, for your job specifically. A small patch and a full roof are very different days.

## Step 4: On the day

When the licensed removalist arrives, you'll see a few things happen in a set order.

**Signage and controlling the area.** Warning signs go up and the work area is set apart so people and pets stay out. Ask your removalist what you need to do on the day, and keep clear of the work area.

**Wetting down.** The material is kept damp while it's worked on, which helps stop fibres lifting into the air.

**Removing sheets whole.** The idea is to take sheets off in one piece, not smash, cut or drop them. Breaking sheeting creates the dust that makes asbestos a problem. That's also why you shouldn't use a power tool on suspect material, and if you've already drilled into some, our guide on [what to do if you've drilled into asbestos](/blog/drilled-into-asbestos-what-to-do/) is a good next read.

**Wrapping and labelling.** Sheets are wrapped in polythene sheeting about 200 microns thick (about 0.2 mm), sealed with adhesive tape, and each package carries the asbestos warning mark, which reads: CAUTION ASBESTOS DO NOT OPEN OR DAMAGE BAG DO NOT INHALE DUST. Packages should be small enough to be handled easily. Friable material and dust, which is Class A work, go in double polythene bags.

## Step 5: Transport and disposal

Asbestos waste can only go to a landfill authorised to receive it. It never goes in a general skip or the kerbside hard-waste collection. In the Barwon region, the only facility on EPA Victoria's register is Drysdale Landfill, and its published acceptance is for small domestic quantities. Every Surf Coast Shire transfer station refuses asbestos outright. In a vehicle, the load sits on two layers of polythene sheeting, with the packages labelled before transport. Our [guide to asbestos disposal in the Barwon region](/blog/asbestos-disposal-barwon-region/) has the local detail, and our [Geelong disposal page](/asbestos-disposal-geelong/) covers the rest.

Landfills that accept asbestos can still choose not to, and may want a booking, so it's the removalist's job to confirm where the load is going before the day.

## Step 6: Decontamination

After the sheets are gone, the area is cleaned up. Tools and equipment are decontaminated, and any asbestos-contaminated dust is dealt with as part of the job. This is where a careful removalist spends time you can't see from the street, and it's why a small job still costs the minimum.

## Step 7: The clearance certificate

On completion of removal work, and before the area is reoccupied, a clearance certificate from an independent person is required. The independent person must be separate from the removalist and have no conflict of interest.

The exemption: it isn't required where the job was only non-friable material not over 10 m2 in total. So the typical rule on a small fibro patch is no certificate, and on larger jobs there's a visual inspection by the independent person. A visual clearance on a typical residential job runs $165 to $250 in Melbourne-metro pricing.

Air monitoring is a different matter. It's a Class A requirement, in the situations where it applies, not a routine part of a Class B job. Where it applies, a Melbourne contractor's guide gives $250 to $600 per day. For a Geelong homeowner the useful fact is that, at an owner-occupied home, the duty to obtain the certificate sits with the licence holder, not with you. Our [clearance certificate and air monitoring guide](/blog/asbestos-clearance-certificate-air-monitoring/) goes through the detail.

## After the removal

Once the area is cleared, the asbestos job is done. Any rebuilding, re-cladding, re-roofing or repair is separate. That's work you sort out with a registered builder, and a licensed electrician or plumber where needed. Keep those quotes separate so you can see the removal price on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does asbestos removal take?

There's no published duration figure, and it depends on the scope of the job: material, area, access and whether it's walls, a roof or a shed. The only hard timeframes are the WorkSafe notification lead times, which are at least 24 hours for 10 square metres or less of non-friable material and at least five days for anything else. Ask your removalist for a written estimate for your specific job.

### Why can't asbestos removal start tomorrow?

The licence holder must notify WorkSafe Victoria before starting. That's at least 24 hours before for jobs of 10 square metres or less of non-friable material, and at least five days before for any other removal work. The wait is a legal requirement, not a scheduling excuse.

### Do I need a clearance certificate after asbestos removal?

Usually, yes. A clearance certificate from an independent person is needed on completion and before the area is reoccupied, unless the job was only non-friable material not over 10 square metres in total. At your own home, the licence holder must obtain it.

### Where does the asbestos go after it's removed?

It's wrapped, labelled and taken to a landfill authorised to receive asbestos. In the Barwon region, Drysdale Landfill is the only facility on EPA Victoria's register, and asbestos can never go in a general skip or the hard-waste collection.
