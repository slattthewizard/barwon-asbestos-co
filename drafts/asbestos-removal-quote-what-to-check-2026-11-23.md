# Asbestos Removal Quote: What a Proper Victorian Quote Should Show

Two quotes for the same fibro job can sit thousands of dollars apart, and the cheaper one isn't always the worse one. The trouble is that most homeowners can't tell what's been left out. This guide is about reading and comparing an asbestos removal quote, line by line, so you know what you're actually being offered. If you want the dollar figures first, our [asbestos removal cost page](/asbestos-removal-cost/) and the [Geelong cost guide](/blog/asbestos-removal-cost-geelong/) cover typical Melbourne-metro benchmarks. Here we're looking at the paperwork.

## Start with the licence number and class

A proper quote carries the removalist's WorkSafe Victoria licence number and the class of licence, A or B. No number, no quote worth considering. Licences are issued by WorkSafe Victoria (its [asbestos removal licences page](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/asbestos-removal-licences) explains the classes), so check the number with WorkSafe if you have any doubt. Our guide to [licensed asbestos removalists in Victoria](/blog/licensed-asbestos-removalist-victoria/) walks through the check.

The class matters because it limits what the holder can legally take on. Class B covers non-friable asbestos only, which is nearly all residential work: fibro cladding, fences, Super Six roofing, eaves, shed and garage sheeting. Class A covers friable asbestos as well, and anything a Class B holder can do. If your job involves crumbling material, pipe lagging or asbestos-contaminated dust, the quote has to come from a Class A holder. Our [friable versus non-friable guide](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) explains the difference.

## The scope: what exactly is being removed

Vague quotes cause most of the disputes. The scope section should say:

- the material (fibro wall sheeting, Super Six roofing, eaves lining, fence sheets)
- the area, in square metres, or the number of sheets
- whether the material is friable or non-friable
- where it is on the property and how it's reached

If the quote just says "remove asbestos" with a price, ask for the detail. The area matters for two reasons: it drives the price, and it decides how much notice WorkSafe must be given. Without a measured area on paper, you can't compare one quote to another, because you don't know if they're pricing the same job.

## Containment, wrapping and disposal

Look for a line covering how the work is contained and where the waste goes. A removalist should describe wetting down the material, taking sheets off whole where possible, wrapping them in polythene sheeting about 200 microns thick, sealing it with tape and labelling it with the asbestos warning mark. Then it travels to a landfill authorised to receive asbestos.

That last part is checkable. In the Barwon region, the only facility on EPA Victoria's register is Drysdale Landfill, and plenty of transfer stations around the region don't take asbestos at all. If a quote says "disposal included" but doesn't say where, ask. Our guide to [asbestos disposal in the Barwon region](/blog/asbestos-disposal-barwon-region/) has the local detail.

## WorkSafe notification and why the start date isn't tomorrow

The licence holder must notify WorkSafe before the work starts: at least 24 hours before for 10 m2 or less of non-friable material, and at least five days before for anything else. A quote that promises to start tomorrow on a larger job is promising something the removalist can't legally do. A sensible quote states the notice period and an estimated start date once it's been accepted.

The notification duty sits with the removalist, not with you. You shouldn't be asked to lodge anything.

## Clearance certificate: is it in the price, and who gets it?

This line trips people up. For jobs over 10 m2 of non-friable material, a clearance certificate from an independent person is required before the area is reoccupied. The independent person can't be the removalist or anyone working for them, so it's usually a separate line item. For visual clearance on a typical residential job, a Victorian provider publishes $165 to $250.

Here's the part many homeowners don't know: at an owner-occupied home, WorkSafe's compliance code puts the duty to obtain the certificate on the licence holder, not on you. So the quote should say either that the certificate is included or that the removalist will obtain it and what it costs. If a quote is silent on clearance for a job over 10 m2, ask why.

Don't expect air monitoring on a routine Class B job either. It's a Class A requirement, and where it applies a Melbourne contractor's guide puts it at $250 to $600 per day. Our [clearance certificate and air monitoring guide](/blog/asbestos-clearance-certificate-air-monitoring/) goes through when each applies.

## GST and the minimum job charge

Published prices don't agree on GST. Some include it, some add it, and many don't say. Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST. Then compare like with like.

Next, find the minimum job charge. Melbourne-metro benchmarks put the minimum for a removal job at $800 to $1,500. It exists because containment, decontamination, disposal and paperwork cost about the same whether two sheets or twenty come down. It's why a small patch costs more than the per-sheet rate suggests, and why you shouldn't be surprised when a quote for a few sheets of eaves lands near the minimum. For a bigger job, general removal runs about $45 to $150 per square metre in Melbourne-metro pricing. There's no published Geelong-specific pricing, so treat all of these as a starting point.

## Why two quotes for the same job can differ

Before you decide one removalist is ripping you off, check the usual reasons:

- one quote includes the clearance certificate and the other doesn't
- one includes disposal and tip fees and the other adds them later
- GST is in one and not the other
- the areas measured are different
- access is harder on one side of the house, or the removalist is further away (some Melbourne providers price regional work separately)
- one prices the minimum job charge and the other doesn't mention it

Line the quotes up against the checklist above. Often the gap closes once the same things are included in both.

## Red flags

Walk away, or at least ask hard questions, if you see any of these:

- **No licence number.** Or a number the removalist won't let you check.
- **Cash only, no paperwork.** A legitimate job has a written quote, an invoice, and a clearance certificate where one is required.
- **An offer to put it in a general skip.** Asbestos waste can never go in a general waste skip or the household hard-waste collection. It has to go to an authorised landfill, wrapped and labelled.
- **A start date that ignores the notice period.** See the WorkSafe section above.
- **No mention of containment or wrapping.** If the quote doesn't say how the material is controlled, ask.
- **A "removal and new roof" bundle.** Removal and replacement are different jobs. Replacement is domestic building work and is something you arrange separately with a registered builder. Ask for the removal quote on its own, so you can see its price and compare it. A bundle makes both numbers harder to read.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should an asbestos removal quote include?

At minimum: the removalist's WorkSafe Victoria licence number and class, the material and area being removed, whether it's friable, how it will be contained and wrapped, where it will be disposed of, the WorkSafe notice period, whether a clearance certificate is included, the minimum job charge, and a clear statement on GST.

### How do I know if the removalist is licensed?

Ask for the WorkSafe Victoria licence number and check it with WorkSafe Victoria if you're unsure. A legitimate removalist will give it freely, and it should be printed on the quote.

### Why is the minimum job charge so high for a small patch of asbestos?

Containment, decontamination, disposal and paperwork cost roughly the same whether a removalist takes down two sheets or twenty. Melbourne-metro benchmarks put the minimum at $800 to $1,500, and there's no published figure specific to Geelong.

### Do I have to get the clearance certificate myself?

For removal at your own owner-occupied home, the duty to obtain a clearance certificate sits with the licence holder, not with you. A certificate is required for non-friable jobs over 10 m2, and it has to come from an independent person rather than the removalist. Check that your quote says who obtains it and what it costs.
