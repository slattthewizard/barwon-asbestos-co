# Neighbour Removing Asbestos Unsafely: What You Can Do in Victoria

You hear an angle grinder next door and look over the fence. Someone is cutting old fibro sheet with a power saw, a fine cloud is drifting across the yard, and the offcuts are going straight into a skip with no wrapping. Your washing is on the line and your kids are in the backyard.

It's an uncomfortable spot, because it's their property and your health. Here's what's worth doing in the first few minutes, how to raise it without starting a feud, and who to contact in Victoria if the work looks unsafe.

## First, protect your own household

Do the practical stuff before anything else. It costs nothing and you don't need to prove anything to anyone.

- Bring the kids and pets inside and keep them in until the work stops.
- Close your windows and doors, and turn off evaporative cooling or anything else that pulls outside air indoors.
- Take washing off the line if dust is drifting your way.
- Don't hose the dust down, sweep it or vacuum it. That spreads it. Our article on [asbestos dust cleanup after renovation](/blog/asbestos-dust-cleanup-after-renovation/) explains why.
- Take photos and video from your own side, with the date and time noted. Keep it factual: what's being cut, how, and whether anything is wrapped.

You can't tell by sight whether the sheeting is asbestos cement. Plenty of old fibro is, and a well-kept sheet can look much like modern fibre cement, but only a lab test can confirm it. What you can judge is how it's being handled. Power tools, breaking sheets by hand and unwrapped offcuts are the warning signs whatever the material turns out to be.

If you think you've been exposed and you're worried about your health, talk to your GP.

## Talk to them first, if it feels safe

A calm word over the fence solves more of these than any complaint does. Most people next door aren't being reckless on purpose. They may have been told the material is fine, or hired someone cheap, or simply not know.

Keep it short and non-accusatory. Something like: "I'm worried that sheeting might be asbestos. Have you had it tested? Can you stop until it has been?" Offer to point them to the basics, such as how [testing works in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-testing-how-it-works-victoria/). If a builder or tradie is doing the work, it's fair to ask the same of them.

If the conversation turns heated, step back. You don't need to win an argument. You need the work to stop or change.

## Who the rules apply to: homeowner versus builder

This is the part that confuses people, and it affects what you can realistically expect.

Victoria's OHS Regulations apply to employers and self-employed people at workplaces. That includes a builder, a demolition crew, a handyman or anyone being paid to do the job. The 10 square metre and one-hour-per-seven-days caps for unlicensed removal of non-friable asbestos are workplace rules, and both caps must hold at once. Friable material is never covered by that exemption.

A homeowner removing asbestos at their own home is in a different position. WorkSafe Victoria's compliance code says the OHS Regulations don't apply there, because a home isn't a workplace. WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the code anyway, and Asbestos Victoria says removal is best done by licensed removalists. But the caps aren't a law that binds a homeowner at their own place.

So there are really two situations next door:

1. **The owner is doing it themselves.** The regulations generally don't apply to them, though the work may still be unsafe and may still raise waste rules.
2. **A builder, tradie or paid helper is doing it.** Then the workplace rules apply, and cutting with power tools, breaking sheets and skipping unwrapped material are the sort of thing WorkSafe cares about.

You often won't know which is which. That's fine. You don't have to work it out before you report something.

## Reporting unsafe asbestos work in Victoria

If a business or paid worker seems to be doing unsafe asbestos work, you can report it to WorkSafe Victoria. WorkSafe's [compliance code for removing asbestos in workplaces](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/resources/compliance-code-removing-asbestos-workplaces) is the document that sets out what safe work looks like, and it's worth skimming if you want to know what you're comparing against. You don't need to be certain the material is asbestos, and you can describe what you saw from your side of the fence.

Include the address, the date and time, what tools were used, whether the sheets were wetted, wrapped or broken, where the waste went, and your photos. Stick to what you observed. Leave out guesses about the neighbour's motives or finances.

### If it's being dumped

Asbestos waste has strict rules. Under Victorian law it's pre-classified as reportable priority waste, and it must be taken to a landfill authorised to receive it. It can't go in a general waste skip or the kerbside bin. If you see asbestos sheets being tipped in a general skip, left in a reserve or dumped on a nature strip, that's a matter for EPA Victoria. Its [asbestos waste page](https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/asbestos-waste) explains the rules and how to contact them.

In the Barwon region, the only facility on EPA's register is Drysdale Landfill, and it takes small domestic quantities only. A load of sheeting from a whole shed or roof going into a standard skip is a red flag.

## What not to do

- Don't confront someone who's angry or threatening.
- Don't go onto their property or pick up offcuts for evidence.
- Don't sweep up dust that has landed on your side. Treat it as contaminated until you know otherwise.
- Don't post their address or accusations online.
- Don't assume the worst. Some jobs look alarming but are done properly, with wet methods and wrapping.

## Your rights as a neighbour

Neighbours' rights over a dispute like this are mostly a matter for the regulators above, plus your own judgement about the relationship. If you're thinking about compensation, damage to property or a civil claim, that's a legal question and a lawyer is the right person to ask. We can't advise on it.

What we can say is that keeping a dated record is the most useful thing you can do, whichever way things go.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I stop my neighbour removing asbestos?

You can ask, and you can report unsafe work to WorkSafe Victoria if a builder or paid worker is involved. A homeowner doing work at their own home isn't bound by the workplace rules, so there may be less that a regulator can do about the work itself. Dumping asbestos waste is a separate issue and goes to EPA Victoria.

### Who do I report asbestos dumping to in Victoria?

Illegal dumping of asbestos waste is a matter for EPA Victoria, which sets the rules for how asbestos waste must be handled and where it can go. Asbestos waste must be taken to a landfill authorised to receive it, and it can't go in a general skip.

### Is my family at risk if a neighbour is breaking up fibro?

It depends on the material, how it's being handled and how much dust gets airborne, and none of that can be judged from over a fence. Keeping children and pets inside and closing windows while work is under way is a sensible precaution. If you're worried about your health, talk to your GP.

### Does the 10 square metre rule apply to my neighbour?

The 10 square metre and one-hour caps are workplace rules, so they bind a builder or paid worker. They don't apply to a homeowner working on their own home, though WorkSafe recommends following the same code. Friable material is never covered by the exemption.
