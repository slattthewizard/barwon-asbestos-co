# Asbestos Dust Cleanup After a Renovation: Who Can Do It and Why Not You

You've pulled down a wall or cut into some old sheeting, the test result comes back positive, and now there's a fine grey film on the floor, the window sills and the tools. The broom is right there. So is the vacuum. Don't touch either. Asbestos dust is one of those jobs where the obvious clean-up moves are the ones that make it worse.

If the work has only just happened and you're not sure what to do right now, start with our piece on [what to do in the first hour after drilling into asbestos](/blog/drilled-into-asbestos-what-to-do/). This article picks up after that: what the dust actually is, why it can't be cleaned like ordinary dust, and who is allowed to clean it up.

## What counts as asbestos dust

Victoria's rules use the term asbestos-contaminated dust, shortened to ACD. It means dust or debris that contains asbestos, usually left behind when asbestos-containing material has been cut, broken, sanded or demolished. It's different from the sheet itself. The sheet might be intact cement; the dust is what's left on the floor after someone put a saw through it.

You can't tell by looking whether a layer of dust contains asbestos. Plaster dust, cement dust and old insulation dust all look much the same. If the work involved a material that's been confirmed or is suspected to contain asbestos, treat the dust as contaminated until a [NATA-accredited laboratory](/asbestos-testing-geelong/) says otherwise. Samples are analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory, and only a lab test can confirm what's in them.

## Why a household vacuum and a broom make it worse

Sweeping throws fine dust back into the air, where it can hang around and settle somewhere new. A standard household vacuum pulls dust in and then pushes the finest part of it straight out through the exhaust and the bag seams. It also contaminates the machine itself. Wiping dry with a cloth smears and lifts it.

None of that cleans a room. It spreads the problem into the carpet, the ducted heating, the next room and your clothes. If the dust has already been tracked around, shut the door on the affected area, turn off ducted heating or cooling that could move air through it, and keep people and pets out.

## Is asbestos dust cleanup licensed work in Victoria?

Mostly, yes. Under Victoria's rules, removing asbestos-contaminated dust is licensed work, and the class of licence depends on where the dust came from.

- **Class A:** ACD is Class A work in general. A Class A licence holder can also do Class B work.
- **Class B:** ACD that is associated with or derived from the removal of non-friable asbestos is Class B work. Cleaning up the dust left after a licensed removalist takes down fibro sheeting is a good example.

There are limited cases where no licence is needed. WorkSafe Victoria's compliance code lists them as "limited asbestos removal work". For dust, that means one of two situations:

1. The removal work doesn't exceed 10 minutes in total, and the person doing it doesn't perform more than one hour of asbestos removal work in total during a seven-day period.
2. An independent person has determined that airborne asbestos fibre levels are likely to be less than one half of the asbestos exposure standard.

An independent person is Victoria's term for someone who's independent of whoever is doing the work or paying for it, has no conflict of interest, and has the knowledge and experience for the job.

### What this means if it's your own home

Those regulations bind employers and self-employed people at workplaces. WorkSafe's compliance code says that where a person is removing asbestos at their own home, the OHS Regulations don't apply because the home isn't a workplace. The code does recommend following it anyway. The rules become law the moment a tradesperson, a paid helper, a tenant or a rental property is involved.

So the straight answer isn't "it's illegal for you to clean it up". It's this: the 10-minute window is tiny, a renovation's worth of dust is usually well past it, and the people the law was written for are licensed removalists. Asbestos Victoria's own homeowner guidance says removal is best done by licensed removalists who are trained to do it safely. ## How a licensed removalist cleans up asbestos dust

You won't see the detail, because you'll be out of the house, but the method is the reason it costs what it does. A licensed removalist typically uses sealed-off work areas, appropriate protective gear and suitable wet methods and filtered equipment, rather than a domestic vacuum. They clean surfaces, then bag and label the waste. At the end, the area is inspected before anyone goes back in.

Where the job involves non-friable material only, with no more than 10 square metres in total, no clearance certificate is required. Above that, the commissioning party must get one from an independent person before the area is reoccupied. At an owner-occupied home, the duty to obtain it sits with the removalist you engaged, not with you. ## Air monitoring: when it's required and when it isn't

Air monitoring is a Class A requirement, not a routine part of every job. Victoria's regulations don't require air monitoring for Class B work. Where it is required on a Class A job, it's done on completion to verify that the airborne asbestos fibre level is less than 0.01 fibres per millilitre. If the reading is at or above that, a clearance certificate can't be issued and more decontamination has to happen.

So if your dust problem is classed as Class A, expect air monitoring on top of the removal itself. A Melbourne-metro benchmark for air monitoring, where it's required, is $250 to $600 per day. A visual clearance certificate on a typical residential job runs $165 to $250. Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST. There's no published Geelong-specific pricing, and the clearance certificate is always a separate line because it has to come from someone independent of the removalist. Behind all of it sits the usual minimum job charge, which is $800 to $1,500 in Melbourne-metro terms.

## Bagging rules if small amounts are being handled lawfully

Say you have a small, contained amount of dust from a job where the limited-work rules clearly apply, or you're a homeowner following the code at your own place. The packaging rules from Asbestos Victoria are specific:

- Dust goes in double polythene bags approximately 200 microns (0.2 mm) thick.
- Fill each bag only to about half full.
- The loaded weight shouldn't exceed 30 kg.
- Every package carries the asbestos warning mark, roughly 75 mm by 90 mm, reading: CAUTION ASBESTOS DO NOT OPEN OR DAMAGE BAG DO NOT INHALE DUST.
- All containers must display the warning mark before transport.

That's the packaging end of it. Disposal is a separate step. Asbestos waste can only go to a landfill authorised to receive it, and in the Barwon region that's Drysdale Landfill, phone (03) 5251 2935. It accepts small domestic quantities only (maximum 6x4 trailer), and you should call before you go. Our [disposal guide for the Barwon region](/blog/asbestos-disposal-barwon-region/) has the detail.

## Where this goes next

If the dust came from friable material, such as crumbling pipe lagging or badly damaged sheeting, it's Class A with no exemption at any quantity. If it came from intact non-friable sheeting that's been broken, it's usually Class B. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I vacuum asbestos dust with a household vacuum?

No. A household vacuum pushes the finest dust back out through its exhaust and seams, and the machine itself then becomes contaminated. Sweeping and dry wiping also lift dust into the air. Shut off the area and have it cleaned by a licensed removalist instead.

### Is asbestos dust cleanup Class A or Class B work in Victoria?

Asbestos-contaminated dust is generally Class A work. The exception is dust associated with or derived from the removal of non-friable asbestos, which is Class B. A Class A licence holder can do both. Limited cases, such as work that doesn't exceed 10 minutes in total, don't need a licence.

### Do I need air monitoring after asbestos dust cleanup?

Air monitoring is a Class A requirement, not a routine part of Class B work. Where it's required, the airborne fibre level has to be less than 0.01 fibres per millilitre before a clearance certificate can be issued. Ask the removalist whether your job needs it.

### How do I bag asbestos dust?

Asbestos Victoria's guidance is double polythene bags approximately 200 microns thick, filled only to half full, with a loaded weight under 30 kg and the asbestos warning mark on each package. Only a lab test can confirm whether the dust contains asbestos in the first place.
