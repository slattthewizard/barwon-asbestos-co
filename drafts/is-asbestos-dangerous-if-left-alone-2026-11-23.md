# Is Asbestos Dangerous If It's Not Disturbed?

Plenty of older houses have a bit of asbestos cement somewhere: a fence, a shed, some eaves, a laundry wall. Most of the time it just sits there. So when a test comes back positive, the first question is usually the sensible one: does this actually need to come out, or can it stay?

The short answer is that intact, non-friable asbestos cement that nobody is cutting, drilling or breaking is commonly managed in place. The risk comes from damage, from weathering to the point of crumbling, and from disturbance during work. This guide walks through how to decide.

## Why condition matters more than the material

Asbestos only becomes a problem when fibres get into the air. Bonded (non-friable) material such as asbestos cement sheeting holds its fibres in a cement matrix. While it's whole, dry and in decent shape, it's not releasing much. Friable material, which crumbles by hand, is different, and it's why our guide to [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) is worth a read before you decide anything.

Three things change the picture:

- **Damage.** Cracked, broken or hail-damaged sheets, or sheets with drilled or cut edges.
- **Weathering.** Old sheeting that's gone chalky, flaky or crumbly on the surface after decades outdoors. It may start to behave more like friable material.
- **Disturbance.** Sanding, drilling, cutting, scraping, high-pressure washing or demolition. This is where most exposure comes from, and it's how most homeowners get into trouble.

None of this means you can spot asbestos by looking. Modern fibre cement looks almost the same, and only a lab test can confirm what a sheet is. If you're not sure you have asbestos at all, start with [how asbestos testing works in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-testing-how-it-works-victoria/).

## A simple decision guide

Run through these six questions for each material that tested positive.

### 1. What condition is it in?

Intact, painted or sealed, firmly fixed and dry: commonly left in place. Cracked, broken, crumbling or water-damaged: much harder to justify keeping, and more cautious handling is needed. If anything has already been broken and dust is on the ground, see our article on [asbestos dust cleanup](/blog/asbestos-dust-cleanup-after-renovation/) first.

### 2. Where is it?

A sheet on a back fence that nobody touches is a different proposition from a sheet that's inside a hallway, behind a hot water service or at kids' head height in a cubby. Material in a high-traffic spot gets bumped, drilled for a hook, or hit with a ball.

### 3. Is any work planned nearby?

This is the big one. A renovation, a new window, a kitchen, re-roofing, a new deck fixed to an old wall: anything that cuts, drills or removes material next to the asbestos changes the answer. Our guide to [asbestos before renovating or demolishing in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-before-renovating-demolishing-victoria/) covers what to sort out before tradies arrive.

### 4. Are you selling?

Many buyers and their inspectors will flag asbestos. Some owners prefer a clean report before listing. Others disclose it and price accordingly. This is a commercial call, and a test result in hand gives you something solid either way.

### 5. Are there kids or pets?

Children and animals are rough on things. If sheeting is within reach and already showing wear, that tips the balance towards sealing or removing it. If you do have concerns about anyone's health, talk to your GP.

### 6. What does it cost?

Removal isn't cheap, and small jobs aren't much cheaper than medium ones because of the minimum. As Melbourne-metro benchmarks, the minimum job is $800 to $1,500 and a full set of eaves is $1,500 to $2,500. A whole fence is $1,000 to $3,500, and a single garage $2,500 to $5,000. There's no published Geelong-specific pricing, and prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST. Our [asbestos removal cost guide](/asbestos-removal-cost/) goes through the numbers.

## Test it so you actually know

Deciding without a result is guessing. A lab test typically costs $88 to $160 per sample in Melbourne-metro and mail-in terms, and most houses need three to eight samples, because each different material is tested separately. Samples are analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory. A standard house inspection with samples is generally $395 to $900.

Once you know what's what, the decision gets simpler. If the only asbestos is a fence at the back of the block, in good condition, no work planned, you may reasonably choose to keep it and look after it. ## Keeping it in place: the sensible habits

Keeping asbestos where it is only works if you actually look after it.

- **Don't disturb it.** No drilling, screwing, sanding, scraping, cutting or pressure washing. Don't dry-brush moss off a roof.
- **Check it now and then.** Look for new cracks, flaking, impact damage or water staining, especially after storms.
- **Consider sealing it.** A sound surface can sometimes be sealed or painted to reduce the risk of surface fibres being released, which we cover in our piece on [painting and sealing asbestos (encapsulation)](/blog/painting-sealing-asbestos-encapsulation/). It isn't a fix for damaged or crumbling material.
- **Keep a record.** Note what was tested, where it is, the lab report, and photos. Put it in the same folder as your building documents.
- **Tell your tradies.** Before any electrician, plumber, builder or handyman starts work, tell them in writing where the asbestos is. Tradies are the main way intact asbestos gets disturbed.
- **Tell the next owner.** The record should go with the house.

## When removal makes sense

Removal is the right call when the material is damaged or crumbling, when it's in the way of planned work, when it's in a spot where it'll keep getting hit, or when you simply want it gone for peace of mind. For anything beyond small contained jobs, use a licensed removalist. Asbestos Victoria says removal is best done by licensed removalists who are trained to do it safely.

A homeowner at their own home isn't bound by the workplace caps of 10 square metres and one hour per seven days, but WorkSafe recommends following the same code. A whole fence line, garage or roof is far past both caps anyway. For details, see [can I remove asbestos myself in Victoria](/blog/can-i-remove-asbestos-myself-victoria/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is asbestos dangerous if it's not disturbed?

Intact, non-friable asbestos cement that nobody is cutting, drilling or breaking is commonly managed in place. The risk rises with damage, with weathering that makes the surface crumble, and with any work that disturbs it. If you have health concerns, talk to your GP.

### Do I have to remove asbestos from my house?

Not automatically. Whether you have to depends on its condition, where it is and what work you're planning. Material that's damaged, crumbling or in the way of building work is much harder to keep. A lab test and a quote from a licensed removalist will help you decide.

### How can I tell if the asbestos in my house is safe to keep?

You can't tell from the look of it, because modern fibre cement can look the same. Only a lab test confirms whether it's asbestos, and condition and location tell you how to manage it. Cracks, crumbling or exposure to impact all suggest it's time to get advice.

### Should I tell tradies about asbestos in my house?

Yes, before they start. Tell them where it is and give them a copy of your test results. Tradies are one of the main ways intact asbestos gets disturbed, so a written note and a quick walk-through are worth the five minutes.
