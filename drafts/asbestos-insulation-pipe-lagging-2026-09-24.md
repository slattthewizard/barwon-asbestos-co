# Asbestos in Insulation and Pipe Lagging: Why It Is Almost Always the Friable Kind

A plumber lifts a subfloor access panel under a 1960s house and finds a rope-like wrap around the hot water pipe. That one find can turn a straightforward job into something else entirely, because asbestos in insulation and pipe lagging is almost always the friable kind, the kind with no small-job exemption at any quantity.

That matters more than most people realise. A cracked fence sheet and a metre of crumbling pipe lagging can sit in the same house, and the law treats them completely differently. One can sometimes be handled without a licence at a small enough scale. The other always needs a Class A licensed removalist, no matter how little of it there is.

## Where asbestos insulation and pipe lagging hide in an old Geelong home

Fibro cladding and Super Six roofing get most of the attention around here, and fairly so, because Geelong's post-war suburbs were built in the exact decades those products were standard. But insulation and lagging are a separate category, hiding in places nobody thinks to look until a trade opens something up.

Victoria's own asbestos authority lists pipe lagging and boiler insulation as textbook examples of friable material, alongside fire retardant coating on structural steel and sprayed insulation. Its guidance says these friable products have been used since the late 1800s for fireproofing, soundproofing and insulation in commercial and industrial settings, and that some of the same products turned up in houses too, and may still be found in houses built before 1990.

In a home, the usual places are the ones nobody sees day to day. Lagging wrapped around a hot water pipe or a copper pipe run in a subfloor. Insulation packed around a wood heater flue where it passes through a ceiling. Millboard sitting between an old wall heater and the timber behind it. Block insulation inside an electric heat bank cupboard. None of it looks dangerous sitting still. All of it can shed fibres the moment somebody cuts, drills or pulls at it.

## Why lagging and old insulation are friable, not bonded

Most of what people worry about on a Geelong property, fence sheets, eaves linings, a Super Six roof, is non-friable. The fibres are locked into cement or vinyl, and a sound sheet left alone sheds very little. Our guide to [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) covers that difference properly.

Insulation and lagging sit on the other side of that line almost by design. They were made to be soft, to pack into gaps and wrap around irregular shapes, which is exactly what makes them friable: loose enough to crumble into dust with light hand pressure once dry. That is a completely different risk profile to a bonded sheet, and it is why the rules treat them differently the moment they are disturbed.

There is one more product category worth naming honestly. Loose-fill asbestos insulation, sometimes known by the trading name Mr Fluffy, is raw crushed asbestos that was blown or poured into ceiling spaces as insulation. It is a real product and a real risk category. It is also, on the government sources we could verify, a New South Wales story, not a documented Victorian one. We are not aware of a Victorian government source confirming it turns up in local homes, so we are not going to tell you it does. If ceiling insulation in an old Geelong house looks unusual, loose and fibrous rather than the batts or blown cellulose you would expect, that is a reason to get it tested, not a reason to assume either way.

The lagging may be intact and undisturbed until that point. A single metre of it is still Class A work, because the unlicensed exemption never covers friable material, at any quantity.

A solid fuel heater flue in a Bellarine Peninsula holiday house, packed with insulation where it passes through the ceiling, discovered when an electrician went up into the roof space to run new cabling. Nobody had touched it in decades. It still needs the same pathway as a much bigger job: testing first, then a licensed removalist if the result is positive.

Millboard behind an old wall-mounted gas heater in a 1970s home in Newtown, found during a kitchen renovation when the heater was unbolted from the wall. It looks like nothing much, a stiff grey board maybe a centimetre thick. It is exactly the kind of material Victoria's own examples list names as friable.

Victoria's unlicensed removal limit lets non-friable material be removed without a licence only where the area does not exceed 10 square metres and the removal work does not exceed one hour in any seven-day period, and both caps have to hold. Friable material, which is what insulation and lagging almost always are, is never covered by that exemption, at any quantity. ## Testing insulation and lagging before anyone touches it

You cannot tell what any of this is by looking at it. A lab has to identify it, and our [testing page](/asbestos-testing-geelong/) and our guide to [how asbestos testing actually works in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-testing-how-it-works-victoria/) go through the process end to end, so we will not repeat it here.

What is worth saying is how sampling usually plays out when insulation or lagging is involved. Most houses need three to eight samples, because every different material has to be tested separately. Lagging on a hot water pipe, insulation around a flue and a wall sheet in the same house are three samples, not one, and each one runs $88 to $160 depending on the lab and how fast you need the result. A full inspection with sampling for a standard house sits at $395 to $900. A Class A licence is required at any quantity, with no unlicensed threshold at all. Air monitoring applies on Class A work where the regulations call for it, checking that the airborne fibre level on completion is below the required standard. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is all pipe lagging and old insulation asbestos?

No. Plenty of pipe lagging and ceiling insulation installed in Australian homes is fibreglass, mineral wool or another non-asbestos material, and that has always been true even in the decades when asbestos versions were common. The only way to know which one you have is to get a sample tested by a laboratory, not by looking at it or by the age of the house alone.

### If it is asbestos, is lagging or insulation always friable?

Almost always, yes. Victoria's own asbestos authority lists pipe lagging, boiler insulation and sprayed insulation as textbook examples of friable material, and friable material has no small-quantity exemption under Victorian law. Bonded products like fibro sheeting are a separate, non-friable category with different rules.

### Can I remove a small amount of asbestos lagging myself?

There is no unlicensed exemption for friable material at any quantity, so as work it is always Class A, licensed-removalist territory. At your own home the OHS Regulations technically do not apply, because a home is not a workplace, but WorkSafe recommends homeowners follow the same code, and the caps become law the moment a tradesperson, tenant, rental property or paid helper is involved. Our guide to [removing asbestos yourself in Victoria](/blog/can-i-remove-asbestos-myself-victoria/) has the full detail.

Removal has a minimum job charge of $800 to $1,500 regardless of quantity, plus a clearance certificate at $165 to $250 and air monitoring at $250 to $600 per day where it applies.
