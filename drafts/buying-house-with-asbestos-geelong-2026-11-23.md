# Buying a House With Asbestos in Geelong: Test First, Then Decide

You've found a house in Geelong you like. It's older, it's got character, and the price is within reach. Then the building inspection comes back with a line that says "possible asbestos-containing materials". No sample, no result, just a maybe. That's the point where a lot of buyers either panic or shrug, and both are mistakes.

Buying a house with asbestos is common, not unusual, and it's a solvable problem if you get real information early. This article covers what the usual report does and doesn't tell you, how to get samples tested in time, how to use the results when you negotiate, and how to budget for removal if you decide to go ahead.

## Why older Geelong houses so often have it

Asbestos cement was commonly used in Australian homes from the mid-1940s until the late 1980s, and the total ban on all forms came into force on 31 December 2003. The Premier of Victoria's office has said homes built in Australia until 1990 routinely used asbestos products, and that asbestos is estimated to still be present in one in three homes. That's a national figure. Nobody has published a Geelong one.

The local context is about when the houses went up. Geelong's northern suburbs were built in bulk inside that window. The Norlane Housing Commission program peaked between 1951 and 1957 and reached 2,464 houses by 1976, and Corio gained five estates and about 2,500 houses from the late 1950s over roughly twenty years. Much of the Norlane housing was for workers at Ford, International Harvester and a phosphate works.

That's why an older Geelong house is more likely to contain asbestos than one built after 1990. It doesn't tell you what's in the house you're looking at. Extensions, renovations and repairs mean one house can have a mix of old and new material. Our guide on [where asbestos hides in old Geelong houses](/blog/where-asbestos-hides-old-geelong-house/) lists the usual places.

## What a building inspection tells you, and what it doesn't

A standard building and pest inspection is about structure, moisture, roof condition and pests. The inspector can see what's visible, and if they spot fibro cladding, textured ceilings or old eaves lining, they'll often note "possible asbestos".

What they typically don't do is take samples. They can't confirm asbestos by sight, because asbestos cement looks almost identical to modern fibre cement. So the report leaves you with a flag and no answer. That's useful as a prompt and useless as a decision tool.

A line in a report doesn't tell you:

- whether the material is actually asbestos
- which materials, because each one is tested separately
- how much there is
- what condition it's in, which affects whether it's a quiet background issue or a live problem

The only way to answer those is a lab test on samples.

## Get a pre-purchase asbestos inspection with samples

An asbestos inspection before buying house is the step that turns "possible" into a result. A person inspects the property, takes samples from the suspect materials and sends them to a NATA-accredited laboratory for analysis. You get a written report listing what was tested and what was found.

Melbourne-metro benchmarks put a standard house inspection with samples at $395 to $900. There's no published Geelong pricing, so treat it as a guide and ask for a quote. Lab testing per sample typically runs $88 to $160, and most houses need three to eight samples, because each different material is tested separately. Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each provider whether their quote includes GST.

It's a modest cost against a purchase price, and it's far cheaper than finding out after settlement. Call (03) 5550 4817, or [book a test](/#contact). You'll need the agent's or seller's permission to take samples, since it involves a small disturbance of the material. Ask early. Some sellers are happy to let you, and some prefer the inspector to be present.

## Fitting it inside your timeline

This is where buyers get caught. Contracts have timing built in, and finance approval has its own deadline. A test that takes too long is no use.

Standard lab turnaround is one to five business days. If you need it faster, a 24 to 48 hour result generally carries a surcharge of 25% to 50%. That's often worth paying when the clock is tight.

On the legal side, we can't tell you what your contract timing is. Check it with your conveyancer before you book anything: how long you have, what any conditions say, and whether samples need the seller's consent. Do that on the day you sign or even before, because the most common problem isn't the lab, it's finding out too late.

A sensible order of events:

1. Spot the "possible asbestos" flag in the building report.
2. Call your conveyancer about your contract timing, and ask the agent about access for sampling.
3. Book the inspection, with a faster turnaround if needed.
4. Read the report, then decide what to do about price, repairs or walking away.

## Using the results in negotiation

Once you have a result, you're negotiating with facts instead of fear.

**A clear result.** If samples come back with no asbestos detected, you've removed a worry, and you may have a stronger position on the rest of the house. The report is also useful to hold onto for resale.

**A positive result on intact material.** If the asbestos is non-friable and in sound condition, it's often left alone. Many owners live with it for years. You're then buying a house with a known condition, which affects what you'd pay, and what you'd have to do before renovating. Our guide on [asbestos before renovating or demolishing in Victoria](/blog/asbestos-before-renovating-demolishing-victoria/) covers that.

**A positive result on damaged or high-area material.** If sheeting is cracked, crumbling or covers a large area, you've got a known cost coming, and it's reasonable to ask for a price adjustment or have the seller deal with it. Ask your conveyancer how to put that in writing. You'll want it in the contract, not just a conversation.

Get a quote from a licensed removalist before you open the negotiation. A real number carries more weight than "it might cost a lot".

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a building inspection test for asbestos?

Usually not. A standard building and pest inspection will often note "possible asbestos" when it sees fibro or similar materials, but the inspector can't confirm it by sight and doesn't normally take samples. A separate asbestos inspection with samples analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory is what gives you an answer.

### How much does an asbestos inspection before buying a house cost?

Melbourne-metro benchmarks put a standard house inspection with samples at $395 to $900, with lab testing typically $88 to $160 per sample. There's no published Geelong pricing, and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each one whether their quote includes GST.

### How long do asbestos test results take?

Standard lab turnaround is one to five business days, and a 24 to 48 hour result generally carries a surcharge of 25% to 50%. Check your contract timing with your conveyancer before you book so the result arrives when you need it.

### Should I walk away from a house with asbestos?

Not automatically. Intact, non-friable material is often left in place. The decision depends on what the test finds, how much there is, its condition and what you plan to renovate. A written result and a quote from a licensed removalist give you the numbers to decide.
