# Licensed Asbestos Removalist in Victoria: How to Check One Really Is

Anyone can put "asbestos removal" on a ute. What matters is whether the business holds a current WorkSafe Victoria asbestos removal licence, and whether it's the right class for your job. Checking is quick, and a genuine removalist will expect you to do it.

## Ask for the licence number on the quote

Start with the quote. A licensed removalist puts its WorkSafe Victoria licence number and licence class on it. If it isn't there, ask. If the answer is vague, or "we don't need one for this", treat that as your answer and move on.

Licences are issued by WorkSafe Victoria, and its [asbestos removal licences page](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/asbestos-removal-licences) explains the two classes. If you have any doubt about a number, check it with WorkSafe directly. Match the business name on the quote to the licence holder, and confirm the class covers your work. Licence numbers are the thing to compare, not logos, vehicle signage or a "fully insured" line. The removalists we put you in touch with are licensed asbestos removalists whose WorkSafe Victoria licence number appears on every quote. You still do the check yourself.

## Class A and Class B

Victoria has two classes of asbestos removal licence.

**Class B** holders can remove non-friable asbestos, plus asbestos-contaminated dust that comes from removing non-friable asbestos. Non-friable means the material is bonded, such as intact asbestos cement sheeting.

**Class A** holders can do everything a Class B holder can, plus friable asbestos and asbestos-contaminated dust from other sources. Friable material includes things like pipe lagging and some old insulation, and fire-damaged or crumbling sheeting.

In practice, almost all residential work in Geelong is Class B. Fibro wall cladding, fences, Super Six roofing, eaves and shed and garage sheeting are all non-friable. If the job involves crumbling material or lagging, you need a Class A holder, and there's no exemption at any quantity. We've covered the distinction in more detail in our [friable versus non-friable guide](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/), so we won't repeat it here.

A common mix-up: a Class A licence isn't "better" for a fibro fence. It's allowed to do the job, but you don't need it. What you need is a licence that covers the material, held by the business that's actually turning up.

## What Class B removal looks like

A Class B removalist handles the typical fibro job from start to finish: notifying WorkSafe, setting up signage and control of the area, wetting the sheets and taking them off whole, wrapping them in polythene about 200 microns thick, labelling them, taking them to a landfill authorised to receive asbestos, and cleaning up. Our [asbestos removal page for Geelong](/asbestos-removal-geelong/) has an outline of what's involved.

Worth knowing: air monitoring isn't a routine part of Class B work. WorkSafe's compliance code says the regulations don't require it for Class B jobs. It's a Class A requirement, in the situations where it applies.

## The limited-removal exemption for tradies

This is where some operators muddy the water. Under the Victorian rules, a licence isn't required for what's called limited asbestos removal work. For non-friable material, that means two caps that both have to hold: no more than 10 m2 in total, and no more than one hour of removal work in total across any seven-day period. The hour is cumulative across jobs, not per job.

So a sparkie who needs to take out one small sheet to run a cable, or a plumber cutting a hole in a bit of fibro, may be working inside the exemption. It's strictly limited. It never covers friable material. It doesn't turn a "licence-free" removalist into a legitimate choice for a fence line, a garage or a roof, because those are far past 10 m2 and one hour. If someone quotes you for a whole roof and says they don't need a licence, they're wrong.

A note on accuracy for homeowners: those caps are workplace rules. WorkSafe's compliance code says that when a person removes asbestos at their own home, the regulations don't apply, because a home isn't a workplace, though it recommends following the code. But once a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property, a business premises or a paid helper is involved, the caps are law. ## Who notifies WorkSafe

The duty to notify WorkSafe sits with the licence holder. It doesn't sit with you, and it doesn't sit with us. The holder must notify at least 24 hours before starting where the job is 10 m2 or less of non-friable material, and at least five days before for any other removal work. That's why a licensed removalist can't turn up tomorrow on a larger job, and why a "start today" offer on a big job should make you pause.

## Who gets the clearance certificate

A clearance certificate has to come from an independent person, someone with no financial or employment link to the removalist and no conflict of interest. The removalist can't certify its own work, and the independent person can't be a director or employee of the removalist.

A certificate is required on completion of removal work, before the area is reoccupied, unless the job was only non-friable material not over 10 m2. And this is the bit homeowners need to know: at an owner-occupied home used solely for domestic purposes, WorkSafe's compliance code puts the duty to obtain the certificate on the licence holder, not on you. So a good quote says who obtains the certificate and what it costs. For visual clearance on a typical residential job, a Victorian provider publishes $165 to $250. Prices are indicative and GST treatment varies between providers, so ask each removalist whether their quote includes GST. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I check if an asbestos removalist is licensed in Victoria?

Ask for their WorkSafe Victoria licence number and licence class, which should be on the quote. Licences are issued by WorkSafe Victoria, so if you're unsure, check the number with WorkSafe and make sure the licence holder matches the business you're dealing with.

### What's the difference between Class A and Class B asbestos removal?

Class B covers non-friable asbestos, such as bonded fibro sheeting, and dust from removing it. Class A covers friable asbestos as well as everything Class B can do. Most home jobs in Geelong are non-friable and so fall to a Class B holder.

### Can a tradie remove asbestos without a licence in Victoria?

Only for limited removal work, meaning non-friable material up to 10 square metres in total, and no more than one hour of removal work in any seven-day period. Both caps must hold, and friable material is never covered. A fence line, garage or roof is well past those limits.

### Who has to get the clearance certificate at my home?

At an owner-occupied home used solely for domestic purposes, the licence holder has to obtain it, not you. It comes from an independent person, and it isn't required if the job was only non-friable material not over 10 square metres.
