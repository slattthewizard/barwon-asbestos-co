# Asbestos in a Rental Property: What Tenants and Landlords in Victoria Should Do

A lot of the advice online about asbestos is written for people who own and live in their house. Rentals are different. There are two parties, one of them lives there and the other one controls the building, and a bit of maintenance that would be a weekend job for an owner-occupier turns into a question about who is allowed to do what.

This article is for both sides. If you rent and you've spotted a cracked sheet, an old fence or a dodgy-looking patch of lining, start with the tenant section. If you own the rental and you're about to fix something, jump to the landlord section. The legal thresholds underneath are the same either way, so it's worth reading both.

## Why a rental changes the rules

Victoria's unlicensed asbestos limit has two caps, and both have to hold. The removal can't exceed 10 square metres of non-friable material in total, and the person doing it can't do more than one hour of asbestos removal work in any seven-day period. The hour is cumulative across jobs, not per job. Friable material is never covered by the exemption, at any quantity.

Those caps come out of the OHS Regulations, which apply to work, not to a person at their own home. The compliance code says plainly that where someone removes asbestos at domestic premises which is their own home, the regulations don't apply, because a home isn't regarded as a workplace. WorkSafe still recommends owners follow the same code in that situation.

A rental isn't the owner's own home in that sense. Once there's a tenant living in the property, or a paid tradie doing the work, or a rental property being maintained as a business asset, the caps apply as law. The homeowner exemption is about an owner at their own home. It isn't a loophole for a landlord who wants to save a few hundred dollars by pulling down a fibro fence on a Saturday.

In practical terms, that means a landlord shouldn't do asbestos removal themselves at a rental, shouldn't hand it to a handyman who isn't licensed, and shouldn't ask a tenant to do it. The job should be quoted by a licensed removalist, and anything over the caps has to go to one anyway.

## If you're a tenant

You didn't choose the building, and you probably don't know what it's made of. Here's what to do and not do.

**Don't disturb it.** Don't drill, sand, scrape, cut, scrub with a wire brush or knock out anything you suspect might be asbestos cement. That includes hanging a picture on an old fibro wall, patching a hole in the eaves, or taking down a damaged fence panel because it's blowing about. If you've already drilled or cut into it, stop where you are, and read our guide on [what to do if you've drilled into asbestos](/blog/drilled-into-asbestos-what-to-do/).

**Don't remove anything yourself.** Even if the landlord says "just take it down", that's not a sound plan. The material may be fine until it's broken, and then it isn't. A tenant removing material from a rental is exactly the situation where the caps are law, not a recommendation.

**Tell the landlord or agent in writing.** An email or a letter, not just a phone call. Say what you've seen and where (include photos), when you noticed it, and whether it's damaged or crumbling. Keep a copy. A written record protects you if there's any argument later about who knew what and when. If the material is broken and exposed, say that clearly and ask what they intend to do.

**Keep the area clear.** Keep kids and pets away from damaged material, don't sweep it up, and don't use a leaf blower or a pressure washer on it. If you've been near debris, wash your hands and change clothes, and talk to your GP if you're worried about exposure.

**Check tenancy rules with Consumer Affairs Victoria.** What a landlord must do about repairs and safety in a rental, and what your rights are if they don't, is covered by Victoria's tenancy rules, and those are Consumer Affairs Victoria's territory, not ours. Search for their rental information before you take it further. We can't give you legal advice about a tenancy dispute.

If you aren't sure whether what you've found is asbestos at all, our guide to [what asbestos looks like](/blog/what-does-asbestos-look-like/) will help you describe it, but only a lab test can confirm it.

## If you're a landlord

Rental properties built or extended before 1990 may contain asbestos. In Victoria, asbestos cement was commonly used in homes from the mid-1940s until the late 1980s, and the total ban came in on 31 December 2003. That doesn't mean your property has it. It means you shouldn't assume it doesn't.

### Test before maintenance

Before a tradie touches old sheeting, lining, a fence or a roof at a rental, test it. A sample analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory is the only way to know. Most houses need three to eight samples, because every different material has to be tested separately. A fence, an eaves lining and a bathroom wall sheet are three samples, not one.

Testing costs far less than the problems it avoids. If a tradie drills through a sheet that turns out to be asbestos cement, you've got a contaminated site, a worried tenant, and probably a very unhappy tradie. ### Tell tradies before they start

If you've got test results, hand them over. If you haven't, say the property is old and the material is unknown. A plumber cutting into a wall, an electrician running cable through a ceiling or a handyman replacing eaves all need that information before they start, not after they've created dust. If a tradie finds something suspect mid-job, they should stop, and you should expect them to.

### Use licensed removalists

If testing comes back positive and the material needs to come out, get a quote from a licensed removalist. Don't send a handyman, don't offer the tenant a rent discount to do it, and don't try to split a larger job into smaller ones to stay under the caps. The one-hour cap is cumulative across a seven-day period, and a whole fence line, a garage or a roof is well past both limits on its own.

Class B licensed removalists handle non-friable material. If the sheeting is damaged enough to be friable, that's Class A work and always needs a licensed removalist. Our guide to [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) explains the difference.

### What happens on the day

The licence holder has to notify WorkSafe before starting: at least 24 hours before for 10 square metres or less of non-friable material, and at least five days before for any other removal. That's why a job can't usually start tomorrow. For the other legal steps, disposal has to go to a landfill authorised to receive asbestos, and the removalist deals with the wrapping, labelling and transport.

Clearance certificates have a wrinkle at rentals. WorkSafe's general rule is that the person who commissioned the work obtains the certificate, not the removalist. The exception is an owner who commissions removal at domestic premises used solely for domestic purposes, where the licence holder obtains it. Don't guess which side of that line your rental falls on: ask the removalist up front, and get the answer in the quote. A certificate isn't required if the work consisted only of non-friable material not exceeding 10 square metres in total. Our guide on [clearance certificates and air monitoring](/blog/asbestos-clearance-certificate-air-monitoring/) explains how it works.

### What comes after

Removal leaves a gap. A new fence, new cladding, a repaired roof or lining is separate building work, and you sort that out separately with a registered builder. Neither this site nor the removalist offers it as part of the removal.

## A quick way to think about who does what

- **Tenant:** notice it, leave it, photograph it, tell the landlord or agent in writing, keep a copy.
- **Landlord:** test first, give results to every tradie before work starts, use a licensed removalist for anything that comes out.
- **Tradie:** stop if something looks suspect, and don't carry on until it's tested.
- **Nobody:** sands, drills, scrapes or breaks it as a favour or to save money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a landlord remove asbestos from a rental property themselves?

They shouldn't. The homeowner exemption is about an owner working at their own home. Once a rental property, a tenant or a paid tradie is involved, the 10 square metre and one hour per seven days caps apply as law, and friable material is never covered. Anything beyond that, and in practice most jobs, should go to a licensed removalist.

### What should a tenant do if they find damaged asbestos sheeting?

Don't touch, drill, sand or remove it, keep children and pets away, and tell the landlord or agent in writing with photos. Keep a copy of what you send. For your rights around repairs and safety in a rental, check Consumer Affairs Victoria, because tenancy rules aren't something we can advise on.

### Does a landlord have to test for asbestos before maintenance?

This article can't tell you what a landlord is legally required to do under tenancy rules, so check Consumer Affairs Victoria for that. As a practical matter, testing before any work on old material is sensible, because it protects the tenant, the tradie and the landlord. Only a lab test can confirm whether material contains asbestos.

### Who pays for asbestos removal in a rental?

That depends on the circumstances and on Victoria's tenancy rules, so check with Consumer Affairs Victoria or get legal advice. What we can say is that the removal itself should be quoted by a licensed removalist, and the person who engages them contracts with them directly.
