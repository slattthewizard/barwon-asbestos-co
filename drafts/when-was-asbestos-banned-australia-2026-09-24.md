# When Was Asbestos Banned in Australia? What the Dates Mean for Your House

A council building file from 1978 turns up during a pre-sale search on a Highton house, and the seller wants one date to settle the asbestos question for them. It will not. When was asbestos banned in Australia is a simple question with three separate answers, and none of them tell you, on their own, whether the eaves on a particular Geelong house are fibro.

Blue and brown asbestos were banned from use and import in the mid-1980s. White asbestos followed on 31 December 2003, when a total ban covered manufacture, supply, use, reuse, import, transport, storage and sale of every form. What matters for your own house is the decades either side of those dates, not the ban date by itself.

## When was asbestos banned in Australia? The three dates that matter

Asbestos cement materials were first manufactured in Australia in the 1920s. They became a standard building material from the mid-1940s and stayed in common use until the late 1980s, according to [Asbestos Victoria](https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/about-asbestos/what-is-asbestos). That forty-year window, not any single ban date, is why so much of the Barwon region's older housing stock needs a second look before anyone touches it.

The bans themselves came in two stages. Australia banned the use and import of blue asbestos, brown asbestos and asbestos-containing products in the mid-1980s. White asbestos, which was used far more widely in cement sheeting, took longer to go. Its manufacture and import was not banned until December 2003, and the full stop only arrived on 31 December 2003, when every form of asbestos was banned outright, in every use.

One manufacturer's own record adds a useful marker in between those two dates. James Hardie, one of the country's largest fibro cement producers, states in its own asbestos fact sheet that it stopped using asbestos in its building products in 1985 and in its pipes business in 1987. That is a company decision, not a law, and other Australian manufacturers kept using asbestos legally in various products until the mid-1980s ban and, for white asbestos, right up to the 2003 cutoff.

## Why the ban date is not the same as "my house is safe"

The ban date tells you when a product stopped being legally made, imported or sold. It does not tell you when your particular house was built, when an extension went up, or when a fence panel was last replaced.

Asbestos cement was standard in Australian residential building from the mid-1940s to the late 1980s. A house built or substantially extended anywhere in that window is the one that needs attention, and Asbestos Victoria treats 1990 as the practical cutoff year for "may contain asbestos", not 2003. Nationally, the [Victorian Government's own estimate](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/fight-protect-workers-asbestos-never-ends) is that asbestos is still present in around one in three homes. That is a national figure, not a Geelong or Barwon one, and nobody has published a regional version of it, so treat it as context rather than a statistic about your street.

A house built after 31 December 2003 should not have had a new asbestos product installed in it, because none were legally being made or sold by then. That is the one date on this page that genuinely draws a line. Everything built or renovated before it sits somewhere on a spectrum, and only testing tells you where.

## What the ban dates change if you are about to renovate, extend or sell

If your house, or any part of it, was built or extended before 1990, the ban dates do not clear you of anything. They only tell you what could legally have been used at the time. The practical question before a renovation or demolition is always the same one. Has this specific material actually been tested? What year the ban came in does not answer that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was asbestos totally banned in Australia?

The total ban on all forms of asbestos, covering manufacture, supply, use, reuse, import, transport, storage and sale, came into force on 31 December 2003. Before that, blue and brown asbestos had already been banned from use and import since the mid-1980s, while white asbestos remained legal in some products until the 2003 ban.

### Was asbestos banned before 2003?

Partly. Blue asbestos and brown asbestos were banned from use and import in the mid-1980s, well ahead of the full ban. White asbestos, the type most commonly used in cement sheeting, was not banned outright until manufacture and import were stopped in December 2003, with the total ban following on 31 December that year.

### Does my house being built before 1990 mean it definitely has asbestos?

No, and nobody can tell by looking at a building permit or a house's age alone. Asbestos cement was standard in Australian residential building from the mid-1940s to the late 1980s, so a pre-1990 house is a reasonable candidate to check, not a confirmed case. Only a sample analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory can actually confirm whether a specific material contains asbestos.

### Can a house built after 2003 still have asbestos in it?

Yes, if it incorporates an older structure, an extension onto an existing asbestos fence or shed, or reused materials from elsewhere. A newly built home should not contain any newly manufactured asbestos product, because none have been legally made or sold in Australia since the 2003 ban. What it might still contain is something older that was already on the block and was never removed.
