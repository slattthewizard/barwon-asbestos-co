# Asbestos Eaves: How to Tell, and Why a Small Job Still Costs the Minimum

A cracked strip of eaves lining along one wall does not look like a big job. In Melbourne-metro pricing terms, though, a full set of asbestos eaves removal typically runs $1,500 to $2,500, and almost any asbestos removal job carries a minimum call-out of $800 to $1,500 before a single sheet comes down. If you have just noticed damaged, discoloured or crumbling lining under your roofline and you are wondering whether you are looking at asbestos eaves, and what dealing with it will actually cost, here is what is actually known, and what still is not.

## What does asbestos eaves lining actually look like?

Eaves are the part of the roof that overhangs the wall line, and the lining underneath that overhang is the eaves or soffit sheeting. From the mid-1940s until the late 1980s, that sheeting was very often asbestos cement, cut to size on site and fixed with cover strips or battens.

Asbestos cement soffit sheeting often has ventilation holes cut into it, in diamond, slot or hexagonal patterns, to let the roof space breathe. It might be a single flat sheet, a laminated board, or a coloured and painted finish. None of that tells you whether it contains asbestos.

That is the frustrating part. A well-maintained sheet of asbestos cement eaves lining can look almost identical to the fibre-cement products sold today, which have contained no asbestos since the mid-1980s. Cracking, splitting, discolouration or a chalky surface are signs of age and weathering, not proof either way.

## Why Geelong's older homes are full of asbestos eaves

Asbestos cement building products were standard in Australian housing from the mid-1940s through to the late 1980s, and the whole category was banned outright from 31 December 2003. Eaves and soffit sheeting were especially common through the 1970s, often on the same houses that used matching asbestos cement wall cladding.

James Hardie, the country's biggest fibre cement manufacturer, says on [its own asbestos fact sheet](https://www.jameshardie.com.au/ContentfulCMS/General/Asbestos_Fact_Sheet_James_Hardie.pdf) that it stopped using asbestos in its building products in 1985, and in its pipes business in 1987. That date is a useful marker: a product genuinely installed after 1985 should be asbestos-free. Older stock sat in sheds and on trucks for years afterwards, though, and installation work dragged on well past any cutoff date. Nobody can tell brand or age just by looking at a sheet from the ground.

Geelong's northern suburbs were largely built in exactly that window, as Housing Commission estates for workers at Ford, International Harvester and a phosphate works. None of that tells you whether any specific set of eaves contains asbestos. It only explains why the odds are higher here than in a house built after 1990.

## The Victorian DIY rule for asbestos eaves, stated correctly

[Victoria's OHS Regulations](https://www.asbestos.vic.gov.au/builders-and-trades/asbestos-removalists/unlicensed-asbestos-removal) let a tradesperson or business remove non-friable asbestos without a licence only where two caps both hold: the area removed does not exceed 10 square metres, and no more than one hour of removal work is done in total during any seven-day period. Both caps have to hold together, and the hour is cumulative across jobs, not per job.

Here is the part most content gets wrong. Those caps are workplace rules. They bind employers and self-employed people doing removal work, and the compliance code makes clear that a person removing asbestos at their own home is not covered, because a home is not a workplace. WorkSafe still recommends homeowners follow the same code at their own home, and the moment a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property or a paid helper is involved, the caps become law.

A full set of eaves on an average house is well past both limits on its own, which is one honest reason those jobs usually go to a licensed removalist rather than staying a weekend project. If the eaves lining is already cracked, crumbling or damaged, be more cautious again. If you have already drilled, cut or sanded into a suspect sheet, stop work straight away and get it tested before doing anything else.

## Testing eaves before anyone touches them

A lab test on an eaves sample typically costs $88 to $160, depending on the lab and how many samples go in together. Some labs charge a flat fee no matter how many samples you submit, and others discount from the second sample onward, so ask about both before choosing one.

Most houses need three to eight samples, because every different material has to be tested separately. Eaves lining, a fence panel and a bathroom wall sheet are three samples, not one, even if they turn out to be the same product. A full house inspection with sampling generally runs $395 to $900, and standard lab turnaround is one to five business days, with a 25 to 50 percent surcharge for a 24 to 48 hour result.

## What happens once your eaves test positive

If the sample comes back positive, the next step is a quote from a licensed removalist, not a DIY plan. Almost all residential eaves work is non-friable and falls to a Class B licensed removalist, unless the material has already deteriorated badly enough to be treated as friable.

For a removal job over 10 square metres, the licensed removalist must notify WorkSafe at least five days before starting, which is the honest answer to "why can't someone start tomorrow." Smaller non-friable jobs need only 24 hours' notice. Once the eaves are down, a clearance certificate from an independent person is required before the area is reoccupied, and at your own home that duty sits with the licensed removalist you engaged, not with you.

One thing this business will never do is quote or arrange the re-cladding, repainting or repair work that follows removal. That is domestic building work in its own right, and it is always something you arrange separately with a registered builder. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it illegal for a homeowner to remove asbestos eaves themselves in Victoria?

Not automatically. Victoria's 10 square metre and one-hour caps are workplace rules that bind employers and self-employed people doing removal work as their job, and the compliance code confirms they do not apply to someone removing asbestos at their own home. WorkSafe still recommends homeowners follow the same code, and the caps become law the moment a tradesperson, a tenant, a rental property or a paid helper is involved.

### How can I tell if my eaves are asbestos just by looking at them?

You cannot, reliably. A well-maintained sheet of asbestos cement can look almost identical to modern, asbestos-free fibre cement, and cracking or discolouration only shows age, not composition. A lab test on a small sample is the only way to know for certain.
