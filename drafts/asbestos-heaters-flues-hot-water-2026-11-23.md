# Asbestos Heater, Flue and Hot Water Service Risks in Older Homes

An old heater, a wood stove or a tired hot water service is the sort of thing a plumber or gas fitter pulls out in an afternoon. If the house is older, that afternoon can be the one where asbestos gets disturbed. Heat-producing appliances were built with materials chosen to cope with high temperatures, and for a long stretch of the twentieth century, asbestos was one of the go-to choices.

This article covers where asbestos hides in and around old heaters, flues, hot water services and stoves, why some of it is much more hazardous than the sheeting on your walls, and why the right order is test first, remove second, replace third.

## Why heating appliances are a hotspot

Asbestos is heat-resistant and doesn't burn, which is why it ended up wherever things got hot. Asbestos cement was used in building products from the mid-1940s until the late 1980s, and the total ban on all forms of asbestos came into force on 31 December 2003. A house built or extended before 1990 may contain asbestos, and heating gear from that period is one of the places it's commonly reported.

Not every old appliance has it. Some do, some don't, and you can't tell by looking. That's the whole reason testing matters here.

## Where it can be found

**Asbestos cement flue pipes.** A flue carries hot gases from a heater, stove or hot water service up through the roof. Old flues were often made of asbestos cement, a grey, rigid pipe that looks a bit like a thick concrete tube. When it's intact and sound, it's a bonded material. When it's cracked, burnt or crumbling, it isn't.

**Heat-resistant boards behind and under heaters.** Flat sheets and boards were fixed to walls and floors behind wood heaters, stoves and gas heaters to protect the surface from heat. They can look like ordinary fibro or a grey-white board. Tiles or render were sometimes fixed over them, which hides them.

**Millboard.** Millboard is a thin board used as a heat shield behind and under appliances, and often behind the metal back of a fireplace. It's commonly reported to have contained asbestos in older installations, and it tends to be softer and more fibrous than sheeting.

**Rope seals and gaskets.** The door seal on an old wood heater or stove, and the gasket around a flue joint or a firebox, was often a braided rope or flat gasket. Rope and woven seals can shed fibres when they're pulled out, scraped or worn.

**Insulation inside old units.** Hot water services, boilers and some older heaters were lagged or lined with insulating material that may contain asbestos. You'd typically see it only when the casing comes off.

**Pipe lagging on the connected pipes.** The pipes leading to a boiler, hot water service or heater may be wrapped in old lagging. Our guide to [asbestos insulation and pipe lagging](/blog/asbestos-insulation-pipe-lagging/) goes through what it looks like and why it's treated so seriously.

## Why some of this is much worse than fibro sheeting

A bonded sheet of asbestos cement that's intact and undisturbed is one thing. Rope, millboard, gaskets, lagging and loose insulation are often a different category altogether.

Friable asbestos is material that can be crumbled or reduced to powder by hand pressure, and it releases fibres far more easily than bonded sheeting. Many of the items above, especially seals, rope, lagging and internal insulation, are commonly friable. In Victoria, friable asbestos is Class A work. Class A covers friable asbestos, and it always requires a licensed removalist, with no exemption at any quantity. The unlicensed limits that apply to some small non-friable jobs, up to 10 square metres and no more than one hour of removal work per seven days, never apply to friable material. Our guide to [friable versus non-friable asbestos](/blog/friable-vs-non-friable-asbestos/) explains the distinction in more detail.

There's another wrinkle. Heat and age break materials down. A flue that's been cycling hot and cold for decades, or a seal that's been baked, is more likely to have degraded than a wall sheet that's just sat there.

## Before a gas fitter or plumber removes an old unit

This is the point where people get caught. A plumber or gas fitter who's asked to swap an old heater or hot water service has the skill to disconnect it and put in the new one. What they usually don't have is a licence to remove asbestos, and they shouldn't be cutting or breaking suspect materials as a matter of course.

So the order that works is:

1. **Test before anyone touches the unit.** Samples of the flue pipe, any board or millboard behind it, the door and flue seals, and any visible insulation are sent to a NATA-accredited laboratory. Each different material is its own sample.
2. **If anything is positive, don't let the job start.** A plumber or gas fitter shouldn't proceed to cut, scrape or pull out suspect material.
3. **Get a quote from a licensed removalist.** For friable material that means a Class A removalist.
4. **Then, and only then, the replacement appliance goes in.**

The appliance replacement is separate work. Fitting a new heater, hot water service or stove is something you engage a licensed gas fitter, plumber or electrician to do directly. We don't offer it, and we don't quote it.

## Spotting the signs

You can't confirm asbestos by eye, but these are the kinds of things that make a test worth doing:

- A grey, rigid, chalky pipe running from a heater or hot water service to the roof.
- A board or flat sheet behind or beneath a wood heater, with tiles or a sheet metal shield fixed over it.
- A thin, grey-brown fibrous board between the appliance and the wall.
- A braided rope seal around a door, firebox or flue joint, especially if it's frayed.
- Soft, fibrous wrapping around a boiler, cylinder or the pipes leading into it.
- Any of the above in a house built or extended before 1990.

## What to do if something has already been disturbed

If a unit has already been pulled out, a flue snapped or a rope seal scraped away, stop and think before you clean up.

- Don't sweep, vacuum with a household cleaner or hose anything down.
- Keep people and pets out of the room.
- Don't put the pieces in the bin or a general skip. Asbestos can never go in a general waste skip or a council hard-waste collection.
- Take photos, cover the area with plastic if you can do it without stirring anything up, and get the debris tested.

Our guide on [what to do if you've drilled into asbestos](/blog/drilled-into-asbestos-what-to-do/) applies to the same situation. If you're worried about exposure, talk to your GP.

## What testing and removal typically cost

These are Melbourne-metro benchmarks, since there are no published Geelong-specific prices anywhere. A lab test typically runs $88 to $160 per sample, and most houses need three to eight samples because each material is tested separately. A standard house inspection with sampling generally runs $395 to $900. Standard lab turnaround is one to five business days, with a 25 to 50 percent surcharge for 24 to 48 hour results.

On the removal side, a minimum job charge of $800 to $1,500 applies even to small jobs, because the containment and disposal costs are fixed. General removal typically runs $45 to $150 per square metre. A small job around one appliance can sit close to the minimum, and friable work tends to be a specialist job. Asbestos waste must go to a landfill authorised to receive it, wrapped and labelled to the rules. In the Barwon region, the only facility on EPA Victoria's register is Drysdale Landfill, phone (03) 5251 2935, small domestic quantities only. Friable material needs double polythene bags and careful packaging, which is one more reason to leave it to a licensed removalist.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an old heater or flue contain asbestos?

Yes, it's possible. Asbestos cement flue pipes, heat-resistant boards, millboard, rope seals, gaskets and internal insulation were all commonly used around heating appliances in older houses. You can't confirm it by looking, so a sample analysed by a NATA-accredited laboratory is the only way to know.

### Is asbestos in a hot water service or stove friable?

Often it can be. Seals, rope, gaskets, lagging and internal insulation are commonly friable, which means they can crumble to powder and release fibres easily. Friable asbestos is Class A work and always needs a licensed removalist, with no exemption at any quantity.

### Should I get an old unit tested before a plumber or gas fitter takes it out?

Yes. Plumbers and gas fitters are licensed for their own trades, not asbestos removal, and pulling out an old unit can disturb seals, boards and insulation. Test the flue, boards, seals and insulation first, and only proceed once you know the result.

### Who installs the replacement heater or hot water service?

That's separate work. After a licensed removalist has dealt with any asbestos, you engage a licensed gas fitter, plumber or electrician directly to fit the new appliance. We put you in touch with licensed removalists and don't offer appliance installation.
